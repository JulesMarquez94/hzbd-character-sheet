# HAZEBOUND

### The Rulebook

*Confront eldritch machines. Command shifting mechanical aether currents. Forge your
survival in an empire choking on volatile arcane industrialism.*

---

## What this game is

Hazebound is a tabletop roleplaying game for a Game Master and a handful of players. One
of you describes a world and everyone else describes a person living in it, and when the
outcome is in doubt, you roll.

**Everything you roll is two six-sided dice and an attribute.** There is no second system
underneath and no third. An attack, a leap, a lie and a working of magic are the same
throw, judged against a different number.

**Everything you can do is on a card.** Your spells, your weapon's two swings, the
manoeuvre you trained for and the eleven things anyone can do are all cards, and each one
says what it costs, what it hits and what happens. Nobody stops the table to look
something up in a book.

**Everything costs something.** A turn is six Action Points. Magic is Willpower. The road
is Supplies. Being brought back from the dead is most of a fortune. A character is a set
of pools and the interesting question is always which one you are willing to empty.

---

## How to read this book

Chapters One to Four make a character and teach the throw. Chapter Five is the fight, and
it is the chapter you will come back to. Six through Ten are the four things characters
spend their lives doing: casting, carrying, resting and making. Eleven is every word in
the game that means something exact.

**If you are learning the game**, read One, Two and Five and start playing. The rest is
reference.

**If you are running it**, the ladder in 1.7 and the quick reference in Appendix A are the
two pages you want open.

Three kinds of aside are set off from the rules:

> **Designer's note.** A number that is mine rather than the designer's, where a rule was
> settled and its arithmetic was not. Appendix B lists all of them.

Terms in **bold** in the glossary mean exactly what the glossary says, everywhere they
appear. A card never explains a defined term in parentheses: you look it up once.

## Chapter One · The Dice

### 1.1 There are two kinds of roll

**A Roll** is `2d6 + an attribute`, thrown against a difficulty. An Attack Roll, an
Attribute Roll, a Skill Check and an Initiative roll are the same roll wearing four
names. Advantage and Disadvantage ride here and nowhere else.

**A value** is the dice a card prints for damage or for healing. No difficulty, no
verdict. Dice explode here and nowhere else.

### 1.2 The four bands

A Roll is judged by how far it lands from the difficulty, not by the faces:

| Distance from the DC | Result |
| --- | --- |
| 6 or more under | Critical Failure |
| under | Failure |
| equal or over | Success |
| 6 or more over | Critical Success |

On 2d6 that puts both criticals in the tails, which is deliberate. A critical **cannot**
be "a natural 20": a Roll in this game is 2d6 plus an attribute.

A Critical Hit guarantees the hit and maximizes the damage that follows it, until the
next Roll. A maximized damage die is by definition showing its maximum, so it explodes
like any other, and the cascade is intended.

When no difficulty is given, the table calls the band. The call is recorded exactly as a
computed one would be.

### 1.3 Advantage and Disadvantage

Advantage adds **1d4** to the total. Disadvantage subtracts **1d4**. Both stack, each
instance adding another d4, and they cancel each other one for one.

### 1.4 Exploding dice

A damage or healing die that rolls its own maximum explodes: roll one die of the next
category up and add it. An 8 on a d8 throws a d10. A die that explodes into another
maximum explodes again, and a d12 throws another d12. A Roll never explodes: boxcars on
the 2d6 are just twelve.

The ladder is d4, d6, d8, d10, d12, and it is the same ladder Elevate climbs.

> **At the table.** Kaelen is level 1 with a Mind of 6. She casts Bramble Whip at a
> Blightgeist eight metres off: two Action Points, one Willpower and then a Ranged Attack
> against the creature's Defense of 8.
>
> She rolls 2d6 and gets a 4 and a 3. Seven, plus her Mind of 6, is **13**. Thirteen
> against 8 is five over: a success, and not a critical, which would have wanted 14.
>
> The card deals 1d6 + 2 x Mind, so 1d6 + 12. The d6 comes up **6**, its own maximum, so
> it explodes: she rolls a d8 and gets 3. Nine on the dice and twelve from her Mind is
> **21 Sharp damage**.

### 1.5 After the dice stop

A Roll is not over when it lands.

- **Karma.** Spend 1 after seeing the result to add 1d4 to it. Offered only when it
  could actually change the band, so at most 4 short: a d4 cannot rescue a roll that
  fell five under.
- **Cards.** Some cards buy a second look for Willpower and are offered on the same
  terms: a card that adds +1 is offered when the gap is exactly 1.

### 1.6 Contested rolls

Both sides roll and the higher result wins.

### 1.7 Setting a difficulty

Most Rolls in a fight name their own target: an attack is against Defense, and a spell
that is not swung names Reflex or Grit. Everything else is the Game Master's number, and
this is the ladder to set it from.

| Difficulty | DC |
| --- | --- |
| Easy | 9 |
| Moderate | 12 |
| Hard | 15 |
| Demanding | 18 |
| Severe | 21 |
| Formidable | 24 |
| Legendary | 27 |
| Mythic | 30 |

**The ladder runs 9 to 30, three points a rung.** Three is exactly half the six-point
critical band, so each step up is half a critical away from the last.

What that means at the table, rolling your best attribute with nothing helping:

| | 9 | 12 | 15 | 18 | 21 | 24 and up |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Level 1** (attribute 6) | 97% | 72% | 28% | 3% | no | no |
| **Level 5** (attribute 8) | certain | 92% | 58% | 17% | no | no |
| **Level 9** (attribute 10) | certain | certain | 83% | 42% | 8% | no |
| **Level 12** (attribute 11) | certain | certain | 92% | 58% | 17% | no |

**The top three rungs cannot be reached by a bare roll at any level.** Two dice and an
attribute of 11 top out at 23. Formidable and above are for a character who has stacked
something on top: Advantage, Karma spent after seeing the dice, a card that adds or an
attribute pushed past 11 by blood or by a working. That is the point of them. A Mythic
task is not a hard task, it is a task you do not attempt without a plan.

The ladder is fixed and the character climbs it. Roughly every four levels a whole rung
falls off the bottom, which is what advancement is for.

Rolling an attribute that is *not* your best moves you one to three rungs down the table,
and each Advantage is worth a little under one rung.

> **At the table.** Dorn wants to talk a dock warden into looking the other way. The Game
> Master calls it **Hard**, which is 15.
>
> Dorn has a Mind of 5 and the Criminal background's Cunning, which grants advantage on
> tricking somebody. He rolls 2d6 for 9, plus 5 is 14, and the advantage d4 comes up 3.
> **Seventeen** against 15: a success by two.
>
> Had he rolled that 14 with no advantage he would have been one short, and the Karma in
> his pocket would have been worth spending: a d4 only has to find 1.

## Chapter Two · The Character

### 2.1 The three attributes

Every attribute starts at **4**. Advancement is the only thing that raises one, and the
ceiling is **12**. Gear and effects can push past that ceiling; advancement cannot.

| Attribute | What it is | What it buys |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Physique** | Strength and fitness | 10 Health a point, and the damage of anything heavy |
| **Instinct** | Awareness and spirit | Defense, Initiative and Speed, and the damage of anything quick |
| **Mind** | Raw intelligence | 2 Willpower a point, and the damage of anything cast |

Some cards name a rule instead of an attribute: "this spell uses your highest
Attribute". That is resolved against whoever holds the card at the moment it is used.

### 2.2 What the attributes buy

Every number below is derived. None of them is typed in.

| Stat | Formula |
| --- | --- |
| Maximum Health | `10 x Physique + 10 x Level` |
| Maximum Willpower | `2 x Mind + 2 x Level + 10` |
| Defense | `Instinct`, plus gear |
| Armor | gear only |
| Initiative | `Instinct + Level` |
| Movement Speed | `3 + Instinct / 2` metres |
| Reflex | `Physique + Instinct` |
| Grit | `Instinct + Mind` |
| Maximum Shield | half of maximum Health |
| Action Points | 6 |
| Reaction Points | 6 |
| Karma | 1 per level |
| Magic Burden capacity | `Level + Mind + 10` |
| Carry capacity | `5 kg x Physique`, plus a bag |

Movement Speed is the only one that keeps a decimal. Everything else rounds down.

A full armor set can replace the base of Defense: see 7.2.

### 2.3 Health, dying and death

Health is the only pool allowed below zero.

- At **0 or below** you are **bleeding out**. You are still on the table.
- At **minus your maximum Health**, a second full bar of damage past zero, you are
  **dead**.
- **Stabilize** stops the bleeding: a Mind Roll against the target's negative Health
  divided by 5. On a success they sit at 0 Health and stay unconscious until healed.

### 2.3.1 Coming back

**Death is not always the end.** A character can be raised, and it is paid for in **coins
and Supplies**, scaled to what they were worth: a level 12 hero costs more to bring back
than a level 2 one.

| | Price |
| --- | --- |
| Coins | 5,000 per level of the character being raised |
| Supplies | 100 per level |

That is 5,000 coins at level 1, which is most of a starting purse, and 60,000 at level
12. It is meant to be ruinous. A party that brings somebody back has decided to, and has
given up something else to do it.

**Doing the work is an ability.** Something has to say it can raise the dead, and the
price above is what that ability costs on top of whatever it asks for itself. The Primal
Life family is where such a spell belongs, and there is not one written yet: see section
12.

The other way is to **pay somebody who has it**. Finding them, and finding them willing,
is the party's problem, and it is one the Game Master is allowed to make interesting.

### 2.4 Shield

Shield is damage soaked before it reaches Health, and whatever it cannot absorb carries
straight through. It caps at **half** maximum Health for everybody, and at **all** of it
for a Feral Cursed.

Shield does not keep. A **Long Rest takes all of it**, whatever put it there. A Short Rest
leaves it standing.

### 2.5 Action Points and Reaction Points

**Six of each**, and seven for a Master Trickster, which is the only thing in the game
that moves either ceiling.

- Action Points come back to full at the start of each of your turns.
- Reaction Points are **earned during a round** and spent outside your own turn. A fight
  begins with none, and the turn boundaries never touch them. The only thing that hands
  any over at the bell is the PREPARED enchantment.
- **Anticipate** converts Action Points into Reaction Points one for one. Nothing is
  spent: the points move, and neither pool may pass its own maximum.

### 2.6 Willpower

Nerve and focus. Spent to power abilities and to bend rolls. Comes back in full on a
Long Rest.

### 2.7 Magic Burden

How much worked magic you can carry before it weighs. Capacity is `Level + Mind + 10`.
Worn, held, on a trinket or clipped to a belt, a working weighs the same wherever it is
carried. It stops at the belt: what is in the pack does not count.

**Capacity is a hard ceiling.** You cannot equip past it. There is no overburdened
state and no penalty to play: the last ring simply does not go on. This is the opposite
of carry weight, which lets you pick anything up and punishes you for it, and the
difference is deliberate. Weight is a thing you can be caught out by. Worked magic is a
thing you decide to put on.

### 2.8 Carry weight

Everything you own has a weight in kilograms and it all counts, pack included.

- Capacity is `5 kg per Physique` plus whatever bag you wear.
- **Over capacity**, your Speed is halved.
- **30% over**, you cannot move at all.

Nothing stops you picking a thing up. Being overloaded is a condition you are in, not a
door that is shut.

## Chapter Three · Advancement

### 3.1 Experience

Experience is a lifetime total, never a counter that resets. The cap is **level 12**.

| Level | Total XP | Step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 0 | |
| 2 | 1,000 | +1,000 |
| 3 | 2,500 | +1,500 |
| 4 | 4,500 | +2,000 |
| 5 | 7,500 | +3,000 |
| 6 | 11,500 | +4,000 |
| 7 | 17,000 | +5,500 |
| 8 | 24,000 | +7,000 |
| 9 | 33,000 | +9,000 |
| 10 | 44,000 | +11,000 |
| 11 | 58,000 | +14,000 |
| 12 | 75,000 | +17,000 |

The climb from 11 to 12 is worth seventeen level-1s, so late levels stay an event.

**Who hands it out is entirely the Game Master's.** There are two ways and a table may
use either, or both, or neither:

- **Experience.** A creature carries a suggested value scaled to its level and rank, and
  the Game Master may award it, ignore it or set their own. It is a suggestion and never
  a rate.
- **Milestones.** The Game Master simply offers the level when the story has reached the
  point. The table above becomes pacing rather than bookkeeping.

Nothing in the game requires experience to be counted at all.

### 3.2 What a level grants

| Level | What it hands over |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | A lineage, a background, a talent set and the +2 / +1 spread |
| 2 | A talent rank |
| 3 | An attribute point and a skill |
| 4 | A talent rank |
| 5 | An attribute point and a skill |
| 6 | A talent rank |
| 7 | An attribute point and a skill |
| 8 | A talent rank |
| 9 | An attribute point and a skill |
| 10 | A talent rank |
| 11 | An attribute point and a skill |
| 12 | A talent rank |

Every even level buys a talent rank. Every odd level after the first gives an attribute
point and a skill learned from the whole codex.

A character who never takes an attribute from anywhere else tops out at **11** in their
best attribute. Reaching 12 takes a lineage, a working or a talent.

### 3.3 Talent ranks

| Rank | Title | Earliest level |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Novice | 1 |
| 2 | Adept | 4 |
| 3 | Master | 8 |

A talent rank may be spent raising a set you hold or taking a new one at Novice.

### 3.4 There is no retraining

**What you spent is spent.** A talent rank taken is taken, an attribute point is where you
put it, and a skill learned is a skill you have. A character is a history rather than a
plan, and a choice you regret is part of who they turned out to be.

The sheet can still hand a talent set back, and handing one back returns exactly what it
gave. That is there to correct a mistake made at the keyboard, not to rebuild a character
between sessions.

## Chapter Four · Making a Character

Five steps, and only the first two are choices you cannot revisit by playing.

1. **Spread your attributes.** A +2 and a +1, on two different attributes.
2. **Choose a lineage.** What you are, and the cards your blood gives you.
3. **Choose a background.** What you did before this, and the skills, kit and purse it
   leaves you with.
4. **Choose your first talent set.** What you have trained at, and where every level after
   this one will go.
5. **Spend the purse.** 6,000 coins and 70 Supplies, against Chapter Seven.

Everything a level 1 character has comes from those five. There is no points buy, no
hit die and nothing to roll.

### 4.1 The spread

Both boosts land at level 1 and they must land on **two different attributes**, so a
finished level-1 spread reads 6 / 5 / 4 and never 7 / 4 / 4.

- **The +2**: one attribute, raised to 6.
- **The +1**: a different one, raised to 5.

### 4.2 Lineage

Thirteen: Celestial, Infernal, Fey, Scorchbound, Skybound, Tidebound, Stonebound,
Draconic, Stalwart, Wildheart, Luminary, Undead, Wildkin.

A lineage hands over cards. Some of those cards ask a question of their own (which scale
colour, which of two traits) and the answer is part of the character.

**A lineage costs nothing and forbids nothing.** There is no prerequisite, no drawback and
no budget it spends. It is not a purchase: it is what you are, and it gives what it gives.
Whether being Undead makes a town uneasy is the table's business and not a rule.

### 4.3 Background

Ten: Criminal, Erudit, Military, Outlander, Craftsman, Entertainer, Merchant,
Aristocrat, Investigator, Mercenary.

A background gives:

- **Skills**, between 1 and 3 depending on the background, chosen from that
  background's own pool. There are 32 skills in the codex.

**A skill is never rolled.** There is no skill value and there never will be one. A Skill
Check is a Roll: 2d6 plus the attribute the task calls for, against a DC off the ladder in
1.7. What holding the skill does is *change* that Roll, and usually it grants Advantage.
The skill card says what it does, and the card is the whole of it.
- **A starting kit**, really granted: the items land in the pack and handing the
  background back returns exactly what it gave.
- **6,000 coins** and **70 Supplies** to start. Every background starts on the same
  purse, whatever else it gives.

The trade is skills against kit and purse: a Merchant takes one skill, a Criminal three.

### 4.4 Talent set

Thirty-four sets are named. **Thirteen are built and playable**: Guardian, Mycomancer,
Cauldron Keeper, Enchanter, Draconic Bond, Trickster, Duelist, Feral Curse, Berserker,
Colossus, Arcanist, Alchemist, Pact of Ordenance.

The other twenty-one are on the wall with no cards behind them and cannot be taken:
Brawler, Runebearer, Hemoturgy, Totemic, Painseeker, Dragon Aspect, Virtuoso, Flowing
Fist, Sharpshooter, Wilder, Necromancer, Spellquill, Spellblade, Thaumaturge, Tactician,
Elemental Aspect, Beastbond, Oathbound, Quartermaster, Weaver, Weapon Master.

Some sets hand over more than cards. A set may give you a **creature** you control, a
**form** you can turn into, a **pact** you feed, a **spellbook** you prepare from or a
**cauldron** you brew in.

## Chapter Five · The Fight

### 5.1 Starting a fight

Everybody rolls Initiative: `2d6 + Initiative`, the same Roll as everything else. Enemies
still standing roll too. A body at 0 Health is not in the order, because it is not taking
turns.

Entering a fight sets Action Points to full and Reaction Points to **nothing**, and hands
over whatever your gear gives at the bell.

**A round is one pass through the order.** It begins with whoever is first and ends when
the last body in the order has taken its turn. Nothing is spent or restored by a round
turning over: it is a way of counting, and it is what an Overlord's extra Action Points
are measured against.

**Being surprised costs you the roll, not the turn.** A side caught out rolls Initiative
with Disadvantage. Everyone still acts in the first round, and the ambush buys position
in the order rather than a free go.

**The fight ends when the Game Master says it does.** There is no condition to check: a
fight can end because one side is down, because somebody ran, because a surrender was
accepted or because it simply stopped mattering. Ending it is what clears the order, and
what makes the next Start Combat hand out its gear effects and zero the Reaction pool
again.

### 5.2 Space and distance

**Every distance in the game is a number of metres**, and every card prints the feet
beside it. How the table measures those metres is the table's own business: theatre of
the mind and a battle map are both supported, and no rule assumes either.

The conversion is fixed, and it is the one that lets both work at once:

> **1.5 metres = 5 feet = 1 space.**

So a 9 metre spell is 30 feet, or 6 spaces on a grid drawn in 5 foot squares. A Shove
pushes 1.5 metres, which is one square. Movement Speed at Instinct 4 is 5 metres, which
is a little over three squares.

There is no grid rule beyond the conversion: no diagonals, no facing, no rule for moving
past somebody. A table playing on a map uses the map's own conventions.

### 5.3 A turn

A turn has a **Turn Start** and a **Turn End**.

At the Turn Start:

- Action Points come back to full.
- Every running effect loses a turn. An effect written "3 turns" covers this turn and the
  two after it, so it counts down at the top of yours and not the bottom.
- Upkeeps are paid, and effects that tick, tick.

The Turn End spends nothing and moves nothing.

Reaction Points are untouched by either boundary.

### 5.4 Reactions

A reaction is something you do on somebody else's turn, in answer to what they did. It
costs the same as it would on your turn: you just pay it out of the Reaction pool
instead.

**One reaction per action.** When an action is taken, everyone at the table with Reaction
Points is offered the window. The first to step in takes it, and everyone else's offer
closes. That reaction resolves **before** the action does, and then the action carries on.

- **No chains.** You cannot react to a reaction.
- **A movement reaction is the exception**: it resolves *after* the action, so it never
  holds anything up.
- **When it is done, the table answers one question**: did it make the action fail? Asked
  per target where targets were named, since one answer can save one body and not another.
  A failed action is not taken, and **the cost stays spent**: pressing use was the
  decision, and the reaction only undid the consequence.

**There is no readying and no delaying.** You cannot hold your turn or set a trigger for
later. **Anticipate** is the whole of that idea: bank Action Points as Reaction Points on
your own turn, and spend them on somebody else's when the moment comes.

> **At the table.** Kaelen casts Bramble Whip. Before a die is thrown, everyone with a
> Reaction Point is asked whether they want to answer it.
>
> Dorn says yes first, and that closes the question for everybody else: an action gets one
> reaction. Kaelen's roll waits. Dorn spends two Reaction Points on a Shield Wall and puts
> himself between the vine and its target, and his card resolves in full, dice and all.
>
> Now the table answers: did that make Kaelen's spell fail? It did. The vine hits the
> shield and stops. Kaelen's two Action Points and her Willpower are gone all the same.

### 5.5 The basic actions

Every character can do all of these, whatever they are and whatever they are holding.

| Action | Cost | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Move** | 1 AP | Cover ground up to your Movement Speed |
| **Climb** | 1 AP | Go up a vertical surface at half your Movement Speed |
| **Jump** | 2 AP | Leap your Movement Speed across and your own height up |
| **Investigate** | 2 AP | Mind or Instinct Roll against an enemy's Grit, or a difficulty the GM sets. On a success you learn something worth knowing |
| **Hide** | 2 AP | With no enemy in line of sight, Instinct Roll against the Grit of every enemy. Each one you beat loses sight of you |
| **Grapple** | 2 AP | Physique Roll against the target's Reflex. On a success the target is grappled |
| **Shove** | 2 AP | Physique Attack Roll. On a hit, push the target back 1.5 metres (one space) or knock it prone |
| **Skill Check** | GM sets it, usually nothing | Attempt something that is not an attack and is not certain. Choose the attribute it is made with and roll against a difficulty the GM sets. Any skill you hold that applies is brought along and pays its own price |
| **Interact** | GM sets it | Handle something in the world: a door, a lever, something on the floor |
| **Anticipate** | converts | Move any number of Action Points into Reaction Points, one for one |
| **Stabilize** | 4 AP | Mind Roll against a dying entity's negative Health divided by 5 |
| **Inventory** | 4 AP | Get into your pack. The belt is free to reach |

### 5.6 Attacking

A card that rolls against another entity has exactly two shapes, and there is no third
target number:

- **An Attack Roll**, resolved against the target's **Defense**.
- **A plain Roll**, contested against the target's **Reflex** or **Grit**.

An attack succeeds if the total equals or exceeds the number it is rolled against.

### 5.6.1 Martial Moves

A **Martial Move** is a trained manoeuvre four talent sets teach. It is not an action and
it is never taken on its own: you **add it to a weapon attack** as you make one.

- **You choose it before you pay.** The attack's cost and the move's are paid together,
  as one price, and everything the move does to the swing is settled before the dice.
- **A move costs Willpower.** The Action Points belong to the attack it rides.
- **One to a swing.** Every set that teaches them has a Master talent that allows two.
- **The plain attack only**, until a set's Rank 2 talent widens it to the special one.
- It rides the **attempt**, not the landing: the Willpower goes whether you hit or miss,
  and the half of the card that says "on a hit" is the half a miss loses.

Which moves you know is a hand you choose out of the pool, the way a caster prepares
spells, and a Long Rest can change it. See the sets in Chapter Four.

### 5.7 Damage, in order

For each landing, in this order:

1. **Armor** comes off. Armor is per hit: three landings of 6 against Armor 2 are 12
   through, not 16.
2. **Shield** soaks what is left, and whatever it cannot absorb carries straight through.
3. **Health** takes the rest.

> **At the table.** The Blightgeist has Armor 2 and 5 points of Shield, and Kaelen's 21
> Sharp damage arrives.
>
> Armor comes off first: 21 less 2 is **19 through**. The Shield soaks what it can, which
> is all 5 of it, and is gone. The remaining **14 goes to Health**.
>
> Had the card landed three separate hits of 7 instead of one of 21, Armor would have come
> off each of them: three times (7 less 2) is **15 through**, not 19. Armor is per
> landing, which is why a flurry fares worse against armour than one heavy blow does.

### 5.8 The damage types

Nine, in three families of three.

| Family | Types |
| --- | --- |
| **Physical** | Sharp, Blunt, Force |
| **Elemental** | Fire, Cold, Lightning |
| **Metaphysical** | Sacred, Decay, Psychic |

Every card that deals damage names one of these, and there is no tenth. A weapon's type
is the weapon's, and an enchantment can replace it.

> **On the tenth type.** Three cards once dealt one. Vampiric Touch and Drain Fluids
> dealt Necrotic, and Toxic Toad dealt Poison; all three were retyped to Decay on
> 2026-09-02. Poison survives as a **condition**, which is a different thing from a damage
> type, and the Toad still inflicts it.

### 5.9 Vulnerable and Resistant

Both point at a damage type, and both are absolute rather than cumulative:

- **Vulnerable** to a type: you take **double** damage from it.
- **Resistant** to a type: you take **half**.
- **Neither stacks with itself.** Two sources of vulnerability to Fire are still double,
  not quadruple, and two resistances are still half.
- **They cancel each other out.** Vulnerable and Resistant to the same type is neither:
  you take the damage as written.

### 5.10 Cover

**Total cover** is something solid entirely between you and the target. It breaks line of
sight, and nothing that needs to see the target can reach it.

**Anything less is partial cover**, and it is one rule: attacks against a target in cover
are made with **Disadvantage**. A low wall, a doorway, a body in the way and a rolling
cart are all the same thing.

### 5.11 The world hurting you

Falling, drowning, a burning room, a collapsing floor. Rather than a table with a row per
hazard, environmental damage comes in **three bands**, and the Game Master picks the one
that fits:

**A band is a flat number scaled to the level of whoever it happens to.** No dice: the
world does not roll, it simply hits.

| Band | Damage | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Minor** | 5 x level | A short fall, a scald, a lungful of smoke |
| **Serious** | 10 x level | A long fall, standing in the fire, a caved-in ceiling |
| **Deadly** | 20 x level | A cliff, a furnace, the bottom of the lift shaft |

Because it is flat there is nothing to roll, nothing explodes, and a critical has no part
in it. Armor and Shield still take their turn in the usual order: see 5.7.

What that comes to against the Health a character of that level actually has:

| | Minor | Serious | Deadly |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Level 1** (70 Health) | 5 | 10 | 20 |
| **Level 5** (130) | 25 | 50 | 100 |
| **Level 9** (190) | 45 | 90 | 180 |
| **Level 12** (230) | 60 | 120 | 240 |

**A hazard gets relatively worse as you climb**, not better. A cliff takes a third of a
level 1 and the whole of a level 12. That is the opposite of how a monster works, and it
is what keeps the world dangerous to people who have stopped being frightened of
monsters.

> **Designer's note.** Scaling to level is Jules's ruling. The three multipliers are
> mine. If Deadly should not outright kill at the cap, 15 x level tops out at 180 against
> 230 Health instead.

### 5.12 Healing

Health goes up, capped at its own ceiling. Shield goes up, capped at what that body can
hold.

**Nothing heals on its own.** There is no natural healing, no Health that trickles back
over a quiet week, and time by itself does nothing at all. Health comes back from exactly
three places:

- **A rest.** Half your Health on a Short Rest, all of it on a Long one. Both cost
  Supplies, which makes the crate the real healing resource.
- **A card.** Anything that says it heals.
- **A potion, or somebody working on you in the field.** This is the only healing that
  happens mid-fight without a card being played, and it is what the potion shelf is for.

So a party out of Supplies and out of flasks does not get better. That is the intended
pressure.

## Chapter Six · Magic

### 6.1 The schools

There are 146 spells in the codex, in three schools:

| School | Families | Spells |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Primal** | Flora, Wild, Life, Blood, Death | 52 |
| **Elemental** | Fire, Water, Wind, Lightning, Earth, Magma, Storm, Mud | 44 |
| **Ethereal** | Light, Shadow, Time, Spacial | 49 |

Elemental has four bases (Fire, Water, Wind, Earth) that pair six ways, so the school
closes at ten families. Three of the six compounds are written: Magma, Storm and Mud.

Two spells are Unique and sit outside the schools.

### 6.2 The rungs

**Novice**, **Adept**, **Master** and a **Legendary** rung that one spell reaches.

### 6.3 What a spell costs

Action Points and Willpower, both printed on the card. A spell's attribute is usually
Mind, but a card may name another or name your highest.

### 6.4 The four second halves

Most spells carry an optional second half, and the four names mean four different things:

- **Overcast**: spend more than the spell asks to make it do more. Always optional.
- **Multicast**: spend more to catch more targets with it.
- **Blood Tithe**: pay in Health rather than Willpower, and always in Physique whatever
  you cast with.
- **Upkeep**: a toll paid at every Turn Start to keep the spell running. Miss one and the
  spell ends there.

An Overcast is either a rider on this cast, priced on top of the printed cost, or a
separate spend on a spell that is already running, priced on its own. The opening clause
is the tell: "When casting X" happens inside the cast, "While X is active" does not.

### 6.5 Empowered and Elevated

Two different things, and they are easy to confuse:

- **Empowered** adds a die. 2d6 becomes 3d6.
- **Elevated** grows the die one step. A d6 becomes a d8. It stacks, and it cannot pass a
  d12.

## Chapter Seven · Gear and Coin

### 7.1 Where things go

| Place | How many |
| --- | --- |
| Armor | 3 slots: head, torso, legs |
| Weapons | 2: main hand and off hand |
| Bag | 1 |
| Belt | 3 loops, up to 5 |
| Trinkets | no ceiling |
| Pack | no ceiling but weight |

The belt is free to reach. Getting into the pack costs the Inventory action.

### 7.2 Armor

Armor comes in three families, each in three tiers: Common, Rare and Epic. Filling **all
three slots** with one family changes what Defense is made of:

| Full set | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Light Armor** | Defense is equal to your Reflex |
| **Magic Armor** | Defense is based on your Grit |
| **Heavy Armor** | Defense is increased by half your Armor |

*Armor* is flat damage reduction and comes from gear alone. *Defense* is how hard you
are to hit. They are two different numbers.

Two rulings settle how the set bonuses meet everything else:

- **All Armor counts toward Heavy's half.** Armor is one number wherever it came from, a
  breastplate or a working or a talent, and the half is taken from the whole of it.
- **Flat Defense from gear stacks on top of a set bonus.** A full Light set makes your
  Defense equal to your Reflex, and a shield's points are then added to that. A set bonus
  changes what Defense is built from. It does not close the door on everything else.

### 7.3 Weapons

A weapon is an item that teaches **two ability cards** while it is held. Equip it and you
have the cards, unequip it and they are gone.

The whole wall is one grid: **what an attack costs in Action Points sets what it deals.**

| Cost | Damage |
| --- | --- |
| 2 AP | `1d6 + stat` |
| 3 AP | `2d6 + stat` |
| 4 AP | `2d6 + 2 x stat` |
| 5 AP | `3d6 + 2 x stat` |
| 6 AP | `3d6 + 3 x stat` |

Four families read that grid sideways:

- **Shields**: the base weapon's damage, one rung down, at 1 more Action Point.
- **Paired weapons**: the same rung down with its dice as d4, rolled twice, +1 point.
- **Crossbows**: their own rung's damage for 1 Action Point less, and a Reload.
- **Firearms**: their own rung's damage for 1 Action Point flat, and a magazine.

Which attribute a weapon swings on is the weapon's, not the wielder's choice.

### 7.4 Bags

Seven rungs. A bag does nothing in a fight: it raises the ceiling your weight is measured
against.

| Capacity | Cost |
| --- | --- |
| 5 kg | 100 coins |
| 10 kg | 200 |
| 15 kg | 300 |
| 20 kg | 400 |
| 30 kg | 600 |
| 40 kg | 800 |
| 50 kg | 1,000 |

**20 coins a kilogram**, flat: no rung surcharge and no rarity multiplier. A bag is worth
exactly what it lets you carry.

The three at the bottom are craft, and they get heavier as they get bigger. The four
above are **Pockets**, a pouch folded through itself, and they get *lighter*: a Ninefold
Pocket holds fifty kilos and rides on a belt loop at three hundred grams. A bag's own
weight counts against the room it gives you.

### 7.5 Charges and uses

A card or an item that can only be used so many times says so, and names the rest that
fills it again. "Once per Long Rest" and "three times per Long Rest" are the same rule
counted to a different number.

### 7.6 Money

**Ten coins is about a euro.** That is the whole conversion, and it is there so a Game
Master can price anything in the world by thinking of what it would cost at home and
adding a nought.

A character starts on **6,000 coins**, which is six hundred euros: enough to be equipped
and nowhere near enough to be armoured.

Everyday prices, so the purse has a scale:

| | Coins | About |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A plain meal | 50 | 5 euro |
| A good meal | 200 | 20 euro |
| A letter carried a day's ride | 100 | 10 euro |
| A day's passage by coach | 300 | 30 euro |
| A week's board, nothing fancy | 600 | 60 euro |
| A bored watchman's silence | 500 | 50 euro |

Against that, a Healing Potion is 200 coins and a piece of Runed armor is 3,000. Gear is
the expensive thing in this world and living is not.

> **Designer's note.** The euro anchor is Jules's. The six rows are mine. Lodging has a
> table of its own in 8.2, because where you sleep changes what a rest gives you.

### 7.7 Supplies

Rations, powder, reagents, rope, oil, thread. One number rather than a list, because the
list is the thing the number replaces.

- **Supplies weigh nothing** and take no pack room. Counting them by item is exactly what
  this pool exists to avoid.
- **A Supply is 10 coins**, which is a euro. This is not a proposal: every Novice potion
  in the codex costs exactly ten times in coin what it costs in Supplies to brew, and
  laying an enchantment is 70 Supplies against 750 coins to buy the same working.
- **A character starts with 70**, which is seven nights on the road.

Above Novice the market charges a premium: an Adept potion runs 27 to 60 coins a Supply
and a Master one 20 to 43. That gap is the whole argument for having an Alchemist in the
party rather than a purse.

**Running out is the pressure.** Supplies pay for both rests, and nothing heals without a
rest or a flask (see 5.12), so a party out of Supplies is a party that stops getting
better.

## Chapter Eight · Rest and Downtime

| | Short Rest | Long Rest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Time | 1 hour | 8 hours |
| Supplies | 5 | 10 |
| Health | half your maximum back | all of it |
| Willpower | nothing | all of it |
| Shield | left standing | all of it gone |
| Ends | what says "until a short rest" | that, and what says "until a long rest" |
| Actions | none | **one** |

A Long Rest ends what a Short Rest ends, and never the other way round: an effect written
"until your next Long Rest" survives a nap.

Both rests also hand back whatever was waiting on one: a flask's charges, a card's uses,
a prepared hand of spells.

### 8.1 The long rest action

A Long Rest buys **one** action. What is on offer depends on what the character can do:

- **Preparing**: a caster who chooses their hand re-chooses it.
- **A labour**: several background skills are work done during a long rest and paid for
  in Supplies. Foraging the ground around the camp, keeping vigil, crafting.
- **Enchanting**: an Enchanter lays a working.
- **Brewing**: an Alchemist turns Supplies into potions.

Rests can be made cheaper. The OZ'EM PICK enchantment and the FRUGAL skill each cut 2
Supplies off both rests, and because a working and a skill are **different sources** the
two stack: a Frugal Enchanter wearing a Pick rests for 6.

### 8.2 Where you sleep

A rest on the road eats your own Supplies. A rest under a roof is **bought with coin
instead**, and what you pay for decides what the night is worth.

| | Coins | What the Long Rest gives, on top of the usual |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A common room** | 100 | Nothing. A bed, a door and no Supplies spent |
| **A good house** | 400 | **1 Karma** |
| **A fine house** | 1,200 | 1 Karma, and you begin the day with **Shield equal to your Mind** |

The prices are per person per night, and they replace the rest's Supplies rather than
adding to them. Karma is still capped at one per level, so a night in a good house is
worth most to somebody who spent theirs.

Sleeping rough is always free of coin and always costs the Supplies.

> **Designer's note.** The two rewards are Jules's. The three prices and the size of the
> Shield are mine. The Shield is written as your Mind because that is the phrase the Runed
> armor set already uses for the same idea.

### 8.3 Downtime

A rest is a night. **A downtime turn is a week**, and it is what the calendar does between
one adventure and the next.

A downtime turn buys **one activity**, the way a Long Rest buys one action:

| Activity | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Earn** | Work at what your background knows. **500 coins**, or **1,000** if a skill or background you hold plainly covers the work |
| **Study** | Learn something the party needs to know. The Game Master sets what is learnable and may ask for a Roll off the ladder |
| **Make** | Craft, brew or lay workings at a scale a single night cannot reach: **seven** Long Rest actions' worth of the same labour, paid for as seven |
| **Recover** | Shake off something a rest cannot end. What qualifies is the Game Master's, and it is the only way to be rid of some things |
| **Stand** | Spend the week on people: a contact made, a favour earned, a reputation built or repaired |

A downtime turn assumes food and a roof, so it costs a week's board (600 coins) or the
Supplies to camp for seven nights, and it can be interrupted, in which case it is spent
and nothing comes of it.

> **Designer's note.** The whole of this section is mine except the idea of it. A week is
> the unit because seven Long Rests is the natural thing to compare it to. Every number
> here is worth arguing with.

## Chapter Nine · Making Things

### 9.1 The same-source law

**An effect does not stack with itself from the same source.** Primal Sense on two rings
is one point of Instinct. A point from a lineage and a point from a ring are different
sources and both count.

### 9.2 Enchanting

A working laid on a person or on a piece of gear. Priced off the **Magic Burden** it
carries:

- **70 Supplies** per point of Burden, when laid at a rest.
- **750 coins** per point of Burden, when bought.

An Ephemeral Enchantment lasts an hour and is never stored on the sheet, because a stored
bonus has no way of coming back off.

**Magic Burden is the only limit.** There is no cap on how many workings an Enchanter may
lay, and no cooldown between them. Lay as many as you can pay for. What stops the pile
growing forever is the other end: Burden is a hard ceiling on what a body can *wear*, so
the limit is on carrying the magic rather than on making it.

### 9.3 Brewing

An Alchemist turns Supplies into flasks during a Long Rest, at **10 Supplies per point of
Willpower** the potion is worth. The Willpower column of the potion shelf is what sets
both its brewing cost and its price.

### 9.4 Brews (the Cauldron Keeper's)

A different thing from a potion. A Brew is Ingredients combined on the spot:

- **at least 1 Essence**, which decides what it does,
- **exactly 1 Catalyst**, which decides who it reaches,
- **any number of Infusions** stirred in on top.

It costs the combined Action Point and Willpower cost of everything in it, and it takes
effect immediately. A Keeper gains Novice Ingredients at Rank 1, Adept at Rank 2 and
Master at Rank 3.

### 9.5 Forging

An item made rather than found: a base item plus a working, named by its maker. The
result is an item instance, which is what lets two silver rings hold different workings.

**Anyone may forge, anywhere, if they can pay.** No talent gates it, no skill, no
workshop and no settlement. The cost is the whole of the limit: forging is closer to
commissioning a made thing than to making one at a bench, and the coin is what says
whether you can.

### 9.6 Potions

A potion is bottled magic, and the point of it is who can drink one: **anybody**. No
talent, no school, no attribute and no card in your hand. A party with no caster in it
can still buy the effect.

**Drinking one costs Willpower**, and Action Points, exactly as the card says. The
Willpower column on the shelf is what a potion is worth in every direction at once: it is
what the drinker pays, it is what the price in coin is built from, and it is what an
Alchemist pays in Supplies to brew one at **10 Supplies per point**.

> **The one exception.** Life Tree Tea charges no Willpower. It is the only Legendary
> potion, it gives you the benefit of a Long Rest for 6 Action Points and 8,000 coins, and
> it cannot be brewed at all. Deliberate or not, it stands alone.

## Chapter Ten · Creatures

### 10.1 The three ranks

| Rank | Action Points | Reaction Points | Extra |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Minion** | 6 | 0 | Cannot take reactions at all. Their number is the danger |
| **General** | 6 | 6 | Player strength. Spends its own Willpower |
| **Overlord** | 12 | 12 | **3 Action Points at every player's turn.** The fight itself |

### 10.2 Creatures scale

Almost nothing about a creature is a printed number. A creature is a shape plus a handful
of coefficients, and a level turns those into a stat block, so the same creature can be
dropped into a level 3 fight or a level 9 one.

The curve is **the character's own**:

- **primary attribute**: `4 + 2 + one per odd level from 3`
- **secondary**: `4 + 1 + one per odd level from 3`
- **the third**: 4

plus the creature's own flat spread, which is what makes one slow and enormous and
another frail. A +1 on the primary is what carries a creature to 12 at level 12, where a
character without a lineage tops out at 11.

There are 9 creatures in the bestiary.

## Chapter Eleven · The Glossary

Every defined term in the game. A term means exactly this wherever it is printed.

### What you spend

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Action Points** | What a turn is made of. Every action costs some, and you get six back at the start of each of your turns |
| **Reaction Points** | Earned during a round and spent outside your own turn. A reaction can be paid from these instead of your Action Points |
| **reaction** | Something you do on somebody else's turn, in answer to what they did. Costs the same as it would on your turn. You just pay it from a different pool |
| **Willpower** | Your reserve of nerve and focus. Spent to power abilities and to bend rolls in your favour. Comes back on a Long Rest |
| **Karma** | Spend 1 after seeing a die result to add 1d4 to it |
| **Supplies** | The crate you travel on: rations, powder, reagents, rope. Spent on the road and on anything crafted during a rest |
| **Coins** | Money. What you spend on people rather than on the road |
| **Sacrifice** | When sacrificing a resource, you ignore all reduction, mitigation or prevention effects and subtract the amount directly |

### What you have

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Health** | How much you have left. Past zero you are bleeding out, and a second full bar of damage kills you outright |
| **Shield** | Damage soaked before it reaches Health, and whatever it cannot absorb carries straight through. Caps at half your maximum Health, or all of it for a Feral Cursed. A Long Rest takes whatever is left of it |
| **bleeding out** | At 0 Health or below. You are still on the table, and a second full bar of damage is death |
| **Defense** | How hard you are to hit. Every attack is rolled against it |
| **Armor** | Flat damage reduction, taken off every hit that lands. It comes from gear alone |
| **Reflex** | How fast you answer sudden danger: diving clear of a blast. Physique + Instinct |
| **Grit** | How well you withstand what gets inside you: poison, fear, a mental attack. Instinct + Mind |
| **Initiative** | Added to your roll for turn order when a fight starts |
| **Movement Speed** | The maximum distance you can cover taking the Move or Jump action |
| **Jump Distance** | The maximum horizontal distance you can cover taking the Jump action |
| **Jump Height** | The maximum vertical distance you can clear taking the Jump action |
| **Magic Burden** | How much worked magic you can carry before it starts to weigh. Capacity is Level + Mind + 10 |

### Rolling

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Attack Roll** | A Roll made to hit. It succeeds if the total equals or exceeds the target's Defense or targeted Attribute |
| **skill check** | A Roll made to determine the success or failure of a non-combat or specialized task, adding the appropriate Attribute |
| **contested roll** | Both sides roll and the higher result wins |
| **advantage** | Roll a d4 and add it to your total. Stacks. Cancels Disadvantage one for one |
| **disadvantage** | Roll a d4 and subtract it from your total. Stacks. Cancels Advantage one for one |
| **reroll** | Roll the same check again and take the new result, better or worse |
| **Critical Hit** | A Roll that lands 6 or more above the DC. Guarantees the hit and maximizes the damage, and a maximized die explodes like any other. 6 or more below is a Critical Failure |
| **Exploding Dice** | Roll the maximum on a damage or healing die and it explodes: roll one of the category above and add it. A d12 throws another d12 |
| **Empowered** | Adds 1 to the number of Damage or Healing Dice. 2d6 becomes 3d6 |
| **Elevated** | Grows the die one size category. A d6 becomes a d8. Stacks, capped at d12 |
| **Damage Dice** | The dice an ability names to deal damage |
| **Healing Dice** | The dice an ability names to restore Health |

### Attacking

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Melee Attack** | An attack made within reach, rolled against the target's Defense |
| **Ranged Attack** | An attack made at distance, rolled against the target's Defense |
| **weapon attack** | Either of the two attacks the weapon in your hands teaches you |
| **Martial Move** | A trained manoeuvre bought with a talent, used alongside a weapon attack rather than instead of one |
| **Melee** | A range targeting entities directly adjacent or within physical reach |
| **Ranged** | A range targeting entities at a distance up to the maximum specified |

### Position

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **entity** | Anything that can be targeted: a person, a beast, a construct, a thing that is awake |
| **touch** | An entity or object adjacent to you that you can physically touch |
| **reach** | As far as you can strike without moving your feet. Yours unless the thing in your hands says otherwise |
| **see** | An entity, object or location to which you have a direct and clear line of sight |
| **line of sight** | An unbroken view of the target. Total cover breaks it, and so does darkness |
| **total cover** | Something solid entirely between you and it. Nothing that needs to see it can reach it |

### Time

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Turn** | The active phase in combat assigned to one entity. Each turn has a Turn Start and a Turn End. Effects measured in turns lose 1 turn at each Turn Start |
| **Turn Start** | The moment your turn opens, before you have spent anything. Upkeeps are paid here and effects that tick, tick here |
| **Turn End** | The moment your turn closes, after everything you chose to do with it |
| **Long Rest** | An 8-hour break that consumes 10 Supplies. Fully restores Health and Willpower, takes whatever Shield is left and allows 1 Long Rest Action |
| **Short Rest** | A 1-hour break that consumes 5 Supplies. If uninterrupted, you regain half your maximum Health |

### Conditions

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **blinded** | Cannot see, and so cannot use any ability or spell that relies on seeing |
| **frightened** | Disadvantage on all actions against the one frightening it |
| **poisoned** | Disadvantage on all actions until a Long Rest is completed or any Health is regained |
| **diseased** | Minus 1 to all attributes until a Long Rest |
| **burn** | Vulnerable to Fire damage until a Short Rest |
| **vulnerable** | Takes double damage from that damage type. Does not stack with itself, and cancels Resistance to the same type |

| **stunned** | Cannot take Actions or Reactions until the effect ends |

| **incapacitated** | Cannot move, take Actions or spend Reaction Points |

| **Corpse Carrion** | Takes the caster's attribute in Decay damage at every one of its Turn Starts |

| --- | --- |
| **Overcast** | Spend more than the spell asks to make it do more. Always optional, always your call |
| **Multicast** | Spend more than the spell asks to catch more targets with it |
| **Upkeep** | A toll paid at every Turn Start to keep a spell running. Miss one and the spell ends there |
| **Blood Tithe** | A price paid in Health rather than Willpower, and always in Physique whatever you cast with |

### Brewing

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |

## Appendix A · The one page

Everything a Game Master needs open, in the order it comes up.

**The throw.** 2d6 + attribute, against a number.

| Distance from the DC | Result |
| --- | --- |
| 6 or more under | Critical Failure |
| under | Failure |
| equal or over | Success |
| 6 or more over | Critical Success. Maximizes the damage that follows |

**The ladder.** Easy 9 · Moderate 12 · Hard 15 · Demanding 18 · Severe 21 · Formidable 24
· Legendary 27 · Mythic 30. Nothing above 21 is reachable without help.

**What to roll against.** An attack goes against **Defense**. A spell that is not swung
names **Reflex** or **Grit**. There is no third number.

**Advantage** is +1d4, **Disadvantage** is -1d4. They stack and they cancel one for one.

**A turn.** Six Action Points back at the start. Every running effect loses a turn.
Upkeeps are paid. Reaction Points are untouched.

**The basic actions.** Move 1 · Climb 1 · Jump 2 · Investigate 2 · Hide 2 · Grapple 2 ·
Shove 2 · Stabilize 4 · Inventory 4 · Interact and Anticipate variable · a Skill Check
whatever the Game Master says, which is usually nothing.

**Damage lands in this order.** Armor off each landing, then Shield, then Health.
Vulnerable doubles, Resistant halves, neither stacks and the two cancel.

**The nine types.** Sharp, Blunt, Force · Fire, Cold, Lightning · Sacred, Decay, Psychic.

**Death.** 0 or below is bleeding out. Minus your maximum Health is dead. Stabilize is a
Mind Roll against their negative Health divided by 5.

**Rests.** Short: 1 hour, 5 Supplies, half your Health. Long: 8 hours, 10 Supplies, all
your Health and Willpower, no Shield left and one Long Rest action.

**Distance.** 1.5 metres = 5 feet = 1 space.

**Money.** 10 coins is about a euro. A Supply is 10 coins.

**The world hurting you.** Minor 5 x level · Serious 10 x level · Deadly 20 x level.

### Colophon

Written 2026-09-02, from the game rather than about it. The formulas came out of
`src/lib/characterModel.js`, the glossary out of `src/lib/keywords.js` word for word, the
prices out of the codex, and the rulings out of the design log in `data/README.md`. Where
a number could be derived it was derived: a Supply is ten coins because every Novice
potion in the codex is priced at exactly that, not because anybody chose it here.

Sixteen questions the game had never had to answer were put to its designer the same day
and all sixteen came back. This is the first edition that can be read at a table.

the two is wrong.*

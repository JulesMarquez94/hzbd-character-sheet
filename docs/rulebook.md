# HAZEBOUND

### The Rulebook

*Confront eldritch machines. Command shifting mechanical aether currents. Forge your
survival in an empire choking on volatile arcane industrialism.*

---

## What this game is

Hazebound is a tabletop roleplaying game. One player is the **Game Master**. They describe
the world, play everyone who lives in it and decide what happens when the rules do not
say. Every other player makes one **character** and decides what that character does. When
what a character tries could fail, the dice decide.

Three ideas run through the whole game.

**One roll.** Whenever the outcome of something is in doubt, you roll two six-sided dice
and add one of your three attributes. Your total is compared to a difficulty number, which
the Game Master sets or the rules name, and you succeed if you reach it. The roll goes by
three names depending on what it is for: a Skill Check, an Attack Roll or an Attribute
Roll. The dice and the arithmetic are the same for all three.

**Everything you can do is written on a card.** Your spells, the two attacks your weapon
gives you, the moves your training taught you and the basic actions every character can
take are all cards. A card states what it costs, how far it reaches, who it affects and
what happens. If you hold the card, you know the rule. Nothing has to be looked up in the
middle of play.

**Everything has a price.** Acting on your turn spends Action Points, and you get six of
them back at the start of every turn. Casting and other feats of will spend Willpower,
which only comes back when you rest. Resting spends Supplies, which you buy or gather.
Managing those pools is most of the strategy of the game: what you spend now is what you
will not have later.

The rules are played on the Hazebound character sheet, which does the arithmetic for you.
Every number this book describes as derived is worked out by the sheet, and every card is
printed on it with your own numbers filled in. You can play with pen and paper, but the
book assumes the sheet is open.

---

## How to read this book

The chapters are in the order you will need them.

- **Chapters One to Four** teach the roll, explain what a character is made of, describe
  how characters grow and walk you through making one.
- **Chapter Five** is the fight. It is the chapter you will come back to most often.
- **Chapters Six to Nine** cover the four things characters spend their time on: casting
  magic, carrying and buying gear, resting and making things.
- **Chapter Ten** explains how creatures work.
- **Chapter Eleven** is for the Game Master: how to run the game and how to use the
  campaign tools on the website.
- **Chapter Twelve** is the glossary. Every word in the game with an exact meaning is
  defined there.

**If you are new**, read Chapters One, Four and Five and start playing. Everything else is
reference you can look up when it comes up.

**If you are running the game**, read Chapter Eleven, keep the difficulty ladder in 1.10
open and use the quick reference in Appendix A.

Two kinds of aside are set apart from the rules:

> **At the table.** A worked example that shows a rule being used, with real numbers.

> **Designer's note.** A number that is a proposal rather than a settled ruling. Appendix
> B lists all of them.

Terms in **bold** in the glossary mean exactly what the glossary says, wherever they
appear. A card never explains a defined term in brackets: look it up once and it means the
same thing everywhere.

## Chapter One · The Dice

### 1.1 The roll

Whenever what your character tries could fail, you make a Roll. A Roll is always the same
thing: roll two six-sided dice, add one of your three attributes and compare the total to
a difficulty number. The difficulty number is called the **DC**.

- **Reach the DC and you succeed.** A total equal to the DC or over it is a success. A
  total under it is a failure. How far you land from the DC decides whether the result is
  also a critical one (see 1.5).
- **The attribute is Physique, Instinct or Mind.** Which one depends on the roll. A card
  that asks for a Roll names its attribute. A Skill Check leaves the choice to you and the
  Game Master (see 1.3).
- **The DC comes from one of three places.** A card prints it, the target's own numbers
  supply it, or the Game Master sets it from the ladder in 1.10.

On the sheet, using a card that asks for a Roll rolls it for you and shows the total and
the result. Any Roll can also be made by hand from the dice tray.

**Damage and healing dice are not a Roll.** When a card tells you to roll dice for damage
or for healing, such as `2d6 + Mind`, there is no DC and no success or failure. You roll
the dice, add the number and that is the result. Those dice can explode (see 1.7). The two
dice of a Roll never do.

### 1.2 The three kinds of roll

Every Roll is one of three kinds. The kind says what the roll is for and what number it is
compared to. Nothing else changes: the dice, the attribute and the four results are the
same for all three.

| Kind of roll | When you make it | Rolled against |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Skill Check** | You try something that is not an attack and could fail: picking a lock, climbing a wall, talking a guard round, reading an inscription in a dead language. You choose the attribute the task calls for, and a skill you hold that applies can help | A DC the Game Master sets from the ladder in 1.10 |
| **Attack Roll** | You attack with a weapon, or with a spell or ability whose card says it is an attack | The target's **Defense** |
| **Attribute Roll** | A card asks for a Roll and names the attribute: a Physique Roll to grapple, a Mind Roll to stop a friend dying, a spell the target resists rather than dodges | The target's **Reflex** or **Grit**, or a DC the card or the Game Master sets |

**Initiative is a Roll with no DC.** When a fight starts, everyone rolls `2d6 + Initiative`
and turns are taken from the highest total down (see 5.1). Nobody fails it.

When the sheet rolls for you, the table log names the kind of roll it was. When you roll
from the dice tray, you pick the kind yourself and give the roll a name, and that name is
what the rest of the table reads in the log.

### 1.3 Choosing the attribute

An Attack Roll and an Attribute Roll name their attribute on the card. A Skill Check does
not. You say how your character goes about the task, and that says which attribute is
rolled. Two characters can attempt the same task with different attributes, and the table
settles which one applies before the dice are thrown.

| Attribute | What it represents | What it covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Physique** | Strength and fitness | Force, endurance and a body that is hard to ignore |
| **Instinct** | Awareness and reflexes | Quick hands, sharp senses, reading people and presence |
| **Mind** | Intelligence and will | Learning, reasoning, memory and knowing what to say |

Two examples of one task rolled in different ways:

- **Picking a lock.** A character who learned locks from a locksmith rolls **Mind**: they
  know the mechanism and work it out. A character who grew up on the street rolls
  **Instinct**: they feel the pins give and have done this a hundred times.
- **Intimidating somebody.** A character who looms over them rolls **Physique**. A
  character who knows exactly which threat will land rolls **Mind**. A character who
  carries it on presence and nerve rolls **Instinct**.

On the sheet, the Skill Check card asks which attribute you are rolling and shows what you
are worth on each. Any skill, talent card or lineage card of yours that speaks to the task
is offered beside the choice. Ticking one adds what it grants to the roll and pays what it
costs.

### 1.4 Rolls made against you

Not every Roll is yours. When the world acts on your character, the Game Master rolls and
one of your own numbers is the DC. You do not roll to dodge or to resist. If the roll made
against you falls short, you were quick enough or tough enough.

| Your number | What it represents | What is rolled against it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Defense** | How hard you are to hit | Every Attack Roll made at you: a sword, an arrow, a spell that is an attack |
| **Reflex** | How fast you answer sudden danger. `Physique + Instinct` | A trap's blade, a boulder coming down the passage, a blast you have to dive clear of. The Game Master rolls for the trap against your Reflex |
| **Grit** | How well you withstand what gets inside you. `Instinct + Mind` | Poison in the air, fear, a spell that clouds the mind. The Game Master rolls for the poison against your Grit |

A creature's card says which of the three it is rolled against, exactly as your cards do.
A trap, a poison or anything else without a card has no number of its own, so the Game
Master gives it one (see 11.2.1). If the roll succeeds, the danger lands. Its damage comes
from the bands in 5.11.

### 1.5 The four results

A Roll is judged by how far your total lands from the DC, not by what the dice show.

| Your total, compared to the DC | Result | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 6 or more under | **Critical Failure** | You fail badly. The Game Master may add a complication |
| Under | **Failure** | You do not do it. An attack misses |
| Equal or over | **Success** | You do it. An attack hits |
| 6 or more over | **Critical Success** | You do it exceptionally well. On an attack this is a Critical Hit |

A **Critical Hit** always hits, and every damage die rolled for it lands on its maximum
value. A die showing its maximum explodes (see 1.7), so a Critical Hit is usually a very
large hit.

The dice themselves never make a result critical. Rolling two sixes is a total of twelve
and nothing more. Only the distance from the DC matters.

When there is no DC, because the Game Master has not set one, the Game Master decides
which of the four results happened. On the sheet the dice roller shows four buttons for
this, and the result chosen is recorded exactly as a calculated one would be.

### 1.6 Advantage and Disadvantage

**Advantage** means you roll a four-sided die with your Roll and add it to the total.
**Disadvantage** means you roll one and subtract it.

- Several sources of Advantage each add another d4. Several sources of Disadvantage each
  subtract another.
- One Advantage and one Disadvantage cancel each other. If you have two of one and one of
  the other, you roll a single d4 for the one you have more of.
- The sheet shows how many d4 you are rolling as an arrow on the card: green and pointing
  up for Advantage, red and pointing down for Disadvantage.

### 1.7 Exploding dice

When a damage die or a healing die shows its highest face, it **explodes**: you roll one
more die of the next size up and add it to the total.

- The sizes, in order, are d4, d6, d8, d10 and d12. A 6 on a d6 adds a d8. An 8 on a d8
  adds a d10.
- If the new die also shows its highest face, it explodes again.
- A d12 is the largest die. A 12 on a d12 adds another d12.
- Only damage and healing dice explode. The two dice of a Roll never do.

> **At the table.** Kaelen is level 1 with a Mind of 6. She casts Bramble Whip at a
> Blightgeist eight metres away. The card costs two Action Points and one Willpower, and it
> asks for a Ranged Attack against the creature's Defense, which is 8.
>
> She rolls 2d6 and gets a 4 and a 3. Seven, plus her Mind of 6, is **13**. Thirteen
> against a DC of 8 is five over: a success, but not a critical one, which would have
> needed 14.
>
> The card deals 1d6 + 2 x Mind, which for Kaelen is 1d6 + 12. The d6 comes up **6**, its
> highest face, so it explodes. She rolls a d8 and gets 3. Nine on the dice plus twelve
> from her Mind is **21 Sharp damage**.

### 1.8 After the dice stop

A Roll can still be changed after you have seen the result.

- **Karma.** Spend 1 Karma to roll a d4 and add it to your total. You may do this once per
  Roll. Because a d4 adds at most 4, the sheet only offers Karma when it could still turn
  the result into a success or a critical: a Roll that fell five short cannot be rescued
  by it. You can hold at most one Karma per level. The Game Master hands it out.
- **Cards.** Some cards let you improve a Roll you have already made, usually for
  Willpower. The card says what it adds, and it is offered on the same terms: a card that
  adds 1 is offered when you are exactly 1 short.

### 1.9 Contested rolls

When two characters compete directly, both make a Roll and the higher total wins.

### 1.10 Setting a difficulty

Most Rolls in a fight have their DC written on the card. An attack is rolled against the
target's Defense. A spell that is not an attack is rolled against the target's Reflex or
Grit. For a Skill Check, and for anything else without a number of its own, the Game
Master sets the DC from this ladder.

The ladder has six rungs, from 10 to 30 in steps of 4. The same task can sit on any rung.
A lock is Easy or Mythic depending on the lock, and the rung is the Game Master's answer
to how hard this particular one is. Each of the last three columns follows one task up
the ladder.

| Difficulty | DC | Who manages it | A lock | A climb | Talking someone round |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Easy** | 10 | Most people | A latch you push up from the other side | A rope, or a wall with good holds | A friendly innkeeper |
| **Moderate** | 14 | Anyone with a little skill | A plain household lock | A rough stone wall | A dock warden on a slow night |
| **Hard** | 18 | Trained people, and not every time | A good lock on a strongroom door | A wet wall in the rain | A mob that wants blood |
| **Demanding** | 22 | A specialist | A locksmith's own lock, with pins made to seize the pick | A sheer cliff face | A guard captain holding your description |
| **Formidable** | 26 | Nobody unaided. It takes Advantage, Karma or magic | A vault lock nobody outside the guild has seen inside | An overhang of smooth ice | A fanatic, out of their faith |
| **Mythic** | 30 | The stuff of stories | A door sealed by a spell, where the slightest slip sets the spell off | A glass tower in a storm | A king, out of his crown |

The table below shows the chance of success for a character rolling their best attribute
with nothing else helping. Attributes start at 4 and the best one reaches 6 at level 1,
then rises by one at every odd level.

| | 10 | 14 | 18 | 22 | 26 | 30 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Level 1** (attribute 6) | 92% | 42% | 3% | no | no | no |
| **Level 5** (attribute 8) | certain | 72% | 17% | no | no | no |
| **Level 9** (attribute 10) | certain | 92% | 42% | 3% | no | no |
| **Level 12** (attribute 11) | certain | 97% | 58% | 8% | no | no |

Three things follow from that table.

- **The two top rungs cannot be reached by a bare roll at any level.** Two dice and an
  attribute of 11 add up to 23 at most. A Formidable or Mythic task needs something
  stacked on top: Advantage, Karma spent after seeing the dice, a card that adds to the
  Roll, or an attribute pushed past 11 by a lineage or an enchantment.
- **The ladder does not move. The character climbs it.** Your best attribute rises by one
  every two levels, so it takes eight levels to make a whole rung easier.
- **Rolling a weaker attribute costs you.** An attribute two points lower is about half a
  rung harder. Each Advantage die adds 2.5 on average, a little over half a rung.

> **At the table.** Dorn wants to talk a dock warden into looking the other way. The Game
> Master calls it **Moderate**, which is a DC of 14.
>
> Dorn has a Mind of 5, and the Cunning skill from his Criminal background gives him
> Advantage on tricking somebody. He rolls 2d6 for a 6, which with his Mind is 11. The
> Advantage d4 comes up 3. **Fourteen** against 14 is a success, exactly on the line.
>
> Without the Advantage he would have been three short. Three is within reach of a d4, so
> the sheet would have offered him the Karma in his pocket.

## Chapter Two · The Character

### 2.1 The three attributes

A character has three attributes. Every attribute starts at **4**. Advancement raises
them, and the ceiling is **12**. Gear and effects can push an attribute past what
advancement gave it, but never above 12.

| Attribute | What it represents | What it buys |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Physique** | Strength and fitness | 10 Health a point, and the damage of heavy weapons |
| **Instinct** | Awareness and reflexes | Defense, Initiative and Speed, and the damage of quick weapons |
| **Mind** | Intelligence and will | 2 Willpower a point, and the damage of spells |

Some cards do not name an attribute. They say "this uses your highest attribute", and it
is worked out for whoever holds the card at the moment they use it.

### 2.2 What the attributes buy

Every number below is calculated from your attributes, your level and your gear. The
sheet does this for you and nothing here is typed in.

| Stat | How it is worked out |
| --- | --- |
| Maximum Health | `10 x Physique + 10 x Level` |
| Maximum Willpower | `2 x Mind + 2 x Level + 10` |
| Defense | `Instinct`, plus anything your gear adds |
| Armor | From gear only |
| Initiative | `Instinct + Level` |
| Movement Speed | `3 + Instinct / 2` metres |
| Reflex | `Physique + Instinct` |
| Grit | `Instinct + Mind` |
| Maximum Shield | Half of your maximum Health |
| Action Points | 6 |
| Reaction Points | 6 |
| Karma | Up to 1 per level |
| Magic Burden capacity | `Level + Mind + 10` |
| Carry capacity | `5 kg x Physique`, plus your bag |

Movement Speed can end in a half metre. Every other number rounds down.

Wearing a full set of armor can change what Defense is built from. See 7.2.

### 2.3 Health, dying and death

Health is the only pool that can go below zero.

- At **0 Health or below** you are **bleeding out**. You are unconscious and cannot act,
  but you are still alive and still on the table.
- Damage keeps counting below zero. When your Health reaches **minus your maximum
  Health**, you are **dead**. A character with 70 maximum Health dies at -70.
- Another character can **Stabilize** you (see 5.5). They make a Mind Roll against a DC
  equal to your negative Health divided by 5. If they succeed, you stop bleeding out and
  sit at 0 Health. You stay unconscious until something heals you.

### 2.3.1 Coming back from the dead

Death is not always the end. A dead character can be brought back, but it is expensive,
and the cost scales with the level of the character being raised.

| | Price |
| --- | --- |
| Coins | 5,000 per level of the dead character |
| Supplies | 100 per level |

At level 1 that is 5,000 coins, as much as most starting purses. At level 12 it is 60,000
coins.
The price is meant to be ruinous, so that a party which brings somebody back has given up
a great deal to do it.

The price is paid on top of whatever does the raising. Raising the dead takes an ability
that says it can, or a person in the world who has one. Finding that person, and
persuading them, is part of the story.

### 2.4 Shield

Shield is a second pool that sits in front of Health. When you take damage, Shield
absorbs it first, and only what Shield cannot absorb reaches your Health.

- Shield caps at **half your maximum Health**. Some effects raise that cap.
- Shield does not last. A **Long Rest removes all of it**, whatever put it there. A Short
  Rest leaves it in place.
- Shield comes from cards, potions and gear. You never start a day with any unless
  something gives it to you.

### 2.5 Action Points and Reaction Points

You have **six of each**. Some effects can raise either ceiling.

- **Action Points** are what you spend on your own turn. They come back to six at the
  start of each of your turns.
- **Reaction Points** are what you spend on other people's turns. You start a fight with
  none and earn them during the fight. The most common way is the **Anticipate** action,
  which moves Action Points you have not spent into Reaction Points, one for one. Some
  gear grants Reaction Points when a fight starts.
- The start and end of your turn do not touch your Reaction Points. They stay until you
  spend them or the fight ends.

### 2.6 Willpower

Willpower is your reserve of nerve and focus. Spells and many other abilities cost
Willpower, and some cards let you spend it to improve a roll. It comes back in full on a
Long Rest and not before.

### 2.7 Magic Burden

Every enchantment you carry weighs a certain amount of Magic Burden, whether it is on
armor you wear, a weapon you hold, a trinket or an item clipped to your belt. Your
capacity is `Level + Mind + 10`.

**Capacity is a hard limit.** You cannot equip anything that would take you past it.
There is no penalty for being close to the limit and no way to be over it: the last ring
simply does not go on. Items in your pack do not count, because you are not wearing
them.

### 2.8 Carry weight

Everything you own has a weight in kilograms, and all of it counts, pack included.

- Your capacity is `5 kg per point of Physique`, plus whatever the bag you wear adds.
- If you are **over capacity**, your Movement Speed is halved.
- If you are **30% or more over capacity**, you cannot move at all.

Nothing stops you picking something up. Unlike Magic Burden, weight is a state you can end
up in without noticing, and the sheet warns you when you are over.

## Chapter Three · Advancement

### 3.1 Experience

Experience points are a lifetime total. They are never spent and never reset. Your level
is read off the total, and the highest level is **12**.

| Level | Total XP | Step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 0 | |
| 2 | 1,000 | +1,000 |
| 3 | 2,500 | +1,500 |
| 4 | 4,500 | +2,000 |
| 5 | 7,500 | +3,000 |
| 6 | 11,500 | +4,000 |
| 7 | 17,000 | +5,500 |
| 8 | 24,000 | +7,000 |
| 9 | 33,000 | +9,000 |
| 10 | 44,000 | +11,000 |
| 11 | 58,000 | +14,000 |
| 12 | 75,000 | +17,000 |

Each step is larger than the one before it, so late levels stay rare.

**The Game Master decides how experience is awarded.** There are two common methods, and a
table may use either or both.

- **Counting experience.** Every creature in the bestiary carries a suggested experience
  value for its level and rank. The Game Master may award it, adjust it or ignore it.
- **Milestones.** The Game Master grants a level when the story reaches a turning point.
  The table above then only says how far apart those points should feel.

You enter experience on your own sheet, in the experience ledger behind the level badge.
The ledger can also set a level directly.

### 3.2 What a level grants

| Level | What you gain |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | A lineage, a background, a talent set and your attribute spread |
| 2 | A talent rank |
| 3 | +1 on two different attributes, and a skill |
| 4 | A talent rank |
| 5 | +1 on two different attributes, and a skill |
| 6 | A talent rank |
| 7 | +1 on two different attributes, and a skill |
| 8 | A talent rank |
| 9 | +1 on two different attributes, and a skill |
| 10 | A talent rank |
| 11 | +1 on two different attributes, and a skill |
| 12 | A talent rank |

**Every even level** gives a talent rank. **Every odd level after the first** raises two
different attributes by one point each and teaches one skill, chosen from every skill in
the game rather than only your background's.

Advancement alone brings your best attribute to 11 by level 11. Lineages, enchantments
and some talents can add more, up to the ceiling of 12.

### 3.3 Talent ranks

A talent set has three ranks, and each rank unlocks more of the set's cards.

| Rank | Title | Earliest level |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Novice | 1 |
| 2 | Adept | 4 |
| 3 | Master | 8 |

A talent rank can be spent in two ways: raise a set you already hold to its next rank, or
take a new set at Novice. You may hold several sets at once.

### 3.4 There is no retraining

Choices are permanent. A talent rank taken is taken, an attribute point stays where you
put it and a skill learned stays learned. A character is the sum of the choices made along
the way, including the ones you would make differently now.

The sheet does let you hand a choice back, and doing so returns exactly what the choice
gave you. That exists to correct a mistake made at the keyboard, not to rebuild a
character between sessions.

## Chapter Four · Making a Character

Making a character takes a few minutes, and the website walks you through every step.
There are no points to buy, no dice to roll and no arithmetic to do.

### 4.1 Start on the dashboard

On your dashboard, press **Create Character**. It asks for two things: your character's
name and the name of the campaign they are for. Both can be changed later. The character
is created at once, and the creation page opens.

The creation page has two steps at the top: **The character**, which is everything level 1
gives you, and **Their story**, which is the lore. Every choice saves as you make it, so
you can close the page and come back.

### 4.2 The four choices of level 1

The first step shows the level 1 block of your Advancement tab. It has four numbered
panels. Open each one, make the choice and it is written to your sheet.

1. **Talent set.** What your character has trained at. A talent set gives you cards at
   each of its three ranks, and every even level from now on will raise a set or add a
   new one. See 4.5.
2. **Lineage.** What your character is. A lineage gives you a few cards and sometimes an
   attribute bonus. See 4.3.
3. **Background.** What your character did before the game begins. It gives you skills,
   your starting kit and your purse. See 4.4.
4. **Attributes.** A +2 and a +1, placed on two different attributes. See 4.6.

Some choices ask a follow-up question in the same window: which colour your scales are,
what your creature is called, which spells you know. A question left unanswered shows as
a badge on the Advancement tab until you answer it, and you can change any answer later
from the same panel.

### 4.3 Lineage

There are thirteen lineages: Celestial, Infernal, Fey, Scorchbound, Skybound, Tidebound,
Stonebound, Draconic, Stalwart, Wildheart, Luminary, Undead and Wildkin.

A lineage gives you cards. Some are passive traits that are always true of you, and some
are abilities you can use. A few lineage cards ask you to choose between two options, and
that choice is part of the character.

A lineage has no cost and no drawback in the rules. It does not spend anything and it
does not forbid anything. How the world reacts to an Undead walking into a town is for the
Game Master and the story, not for a rule.

### 4.4 Background

There are ten backgrounds: Criminal, Erudit, Military, Outlander, Craftsman, Entertainer,
Merchant, Aristocrat, Investigator and Mercenary.

Choosing a background gives you three things, and the sheet asks for them in order.

**Skills.** Each background has its own pool of skills, and you pick between one and three
of them depending on the background. A Criminal picks three and a Merchant picks one, and
the Merchant starts with more coin to make up for it.

A skill is a card, not a number. **Skills are never rolled.** When you attempt something,
you make a Skill Check, which is an ordinary Roll with whichever attribute the task calls
for (see 5.5). A skill you hold changes that Roll, most often by granting Advantage when
the task falls in its area, and the sheet offers every skill that could apply when you
make the check. The skill card is the whole of what the skill does.

**The starting kit.** After the skills, the outfitter opens. You choose one full Common
armor set (all three pieces) and one Common weapon, or two weapons for a Military or
Mercenary background. Your background also adds a few items of its trade. When you take
the kit, the armor is worn, the weapon is in your hand and the rest goes to your belt and
pack. Nothing else is needed before you can play.

**The purse.** Every background starts with **70 Supplies**. The coins depend on how many
skills the background teaches, because skills are traded against money: **2,000 coins**
for a background with three skills, **4,000** for two and **6,000** for one. Both amounts
land in your coin and Supply ledgers when you take the kit.

If you change your background, the kit is handed back first and everything it gave is
returned.

### 4.5 Talent set

Thirty-four talent sets are named. **Thirteen are written and can be taken**: Guardian,
Mycomancer, Cauldron Keeper, Enchanter, Draconic Bond, Trickster, Duelist, Feral Curse,
Berserker, Colossus, Arcanist, Alchemist and Pact of Ordenance. The rest are shown on the
wall so you can see what is coming, but they have no cards yet and cannot be chosen.

A talent set gives you cards at Novice rank now, and more at Adept and Master as you take
those ranks. Some sets also give you something that is not a card: a **creature** you
control, a **form** you can turn into, a **pact** you must feed, a **spellbook** you prepare
spells from or a **cauldron** you brew in. The set's own page explains it.

### 4.6 Attributes

All three attributes start at 4. At level 1 you place a **+2** on one attribute and a
**+1** on a different one, so a finished level 1 character always reads 6, 5 and 4 in
some order.

The panel shows what each placement buys before you commit: how much Health, Willpower,
Defense, Initiative and Speed each attribute is worth to you.

### 4.7 Their story

The second step is the lore page: a portrait, a description and a journal for notes
between sessions. None of it has rules attached. It is the part of the character nobody
can roll for you, and you can fill it in at any time.

### 4.8 Ready to play

When the four choices are made, open the sheet. The Character tab shows your pools and
your cards, the Inventory tab shows what you carry and the Abilities tab lists every card
you hold, grouped by where it came from.

There is no shop to spend your purse in before the first session. You start with the kit
your background gave you and the coins in your ledger. Anything else you want is
bought in the game, from the people your Game Master puts in front of you. See 7.8 for how
buying works.

## Chapter Five · The Fight

### 5.1 Starting a fight

When a fight begins, the Game Master calls for Initiative. Everyone in the fight makes an
Initiative roll: `2d6 + Initiative`, the same Roll as everything else. Turns are then taken
in order from the highest total to the lowest. Enemies roll too. Anyone at 0 Health or
below is not in the order, because they cannot take turns.

At the start of a fight your Action Points are set to full and your Reaction Points to
**zero**. Any gear that does something when a fight begins does it now.

**A round** is one pass through the whole order. It begins with the first body in the
order and ends when the last one has taken its turn. Nothing is spent or restored when a
round ends. Rounds are only a way of counting.

**Surprise.** A side that is caught unprepared rolls Initiative with **Disadvantage**.
Everyone still acts in the first round. An ambush buys a better place in the order, not a
free turn.

**The fight ends when the Game Master says so.** There is no condition to check. A fight
can end because one side is down, because somebody fled, because a surrender was accepted
or because it stopped mattering. Ending the fight clears the turn order. Running effects
stay on the characters they are on until their own durations end.

### 5.2 Distance

Every distance in the game is written in metres, and every card prints the feet beside
it. The website can print either unit everywhere. The conversion is fixed:

> **1.5 metres = 5 feet = 1 space.**

So a spell with a 9 metre range reaches 30 feet, or 6 squares on a battle map drawn in
5 foot squares. A Shove pushes 1.5 metres, which is one square. A character with Instinct
4 has a Movement Speed of 5 metres, a little over three squares.

The rules do not assume a map. A table playing in their heads and a table playing on a
grid both use the same numbers. There are no extra rules for diagonals, facing or moving
past somebody. A table on a map uses the map's own conventions.

### 5.3 Your turn, step by step

A turn has three parts: the **Turn Start**, the turn itself and the **Turn End**.

**1. Turn Start.** Press **Start Turn** on your sheet, or **Start my turn** when the table
tells you it is yours. The sheet does the following:

- Your Action Points come back to six.
- Every running effect on you counts down one turn. An effect written "3 turns" covers
  the turn it was cast on and the two turns after it.
- Anything that happens at your Turn Start happens now. If you are keeping a spell going
  with an Upkeep, you pay it now or the spell ends. If a condition damages you each turn,
  it damages you now. The sheet stops and asks about each one.

**2. Your turn.** Spend your Action Points on whatever you want, in any order, until they
run out or you choose to stop. Everything you can do is a card on your Character tab:

- **Move** costs 1 Action Point and covers up to your Movement Speed. You can move more
  than once.
- **Attack** with the cards your weapon gives you. A weapon teaches two attacks and each
  prints its cost, from 2 to 6 Action Points.
- **Cast** a spell or use an ability from your talent set or lineage. The card prints its
  cost in Action Points and Willpower.
- **Use** an item on your belt. Reaching the belt is free, but the item's card has a cost.
  Getting something out of your pack instead is the Inventory action, 4 Action Points.
- **Take a basic action** from the list in 5.5: Hide, Grapple, Shove, Investigate and
  the rest.
- **Hold back** with Anticipate, moving Action Points you have not spent into Reaction
  Points so you can act on other people's turns.

When you tap a card, the sheet shows you what it costs and asks whether you are using it
as an action or as a reaction. On your own turn you pick action. The points are spent,
the card's roll is made if it has one and the result is written to the table's log. If a
card would cost more than you have, the sheet tells you and spends nothing.

**3. Turn End.** Press **End Turn**. Nothing is spent by ending a turn. If you were hidden
and did anything at all this turn, you are found. At a table run on the website, your End
Turn is what moves the fight on to the next person.

Your Reaction Points are not touched by either boundary. Whatever you have at the end of
your turn is still there on everyone else's.

### 5.4 Reactions

A reaction is something you do on somebody else's turn, in answer to what they are doing.
A reaction costs exactly what the same card would cost on your own turn. The only
difference is that you pay it from your Reaction Points instead of your Action Points.

**One reaction per action.** When somebody takes an action, everyone with Reaction Points
is offered the chance to react. On the website this is a six-second window that appears on
the sheet of everyone who could answer. The first person to take a reaction closes the
window for everyone else.

- The reaction is resolved **before** the action, dice and all. Then the action carries
  on.
- **You cannot react to a reaction.**
- **A movement reaction is the one exception to the order.** Moving in reaction to an
  action resolves after the action, so it never holds anything up.
- When the reaction is done, the table answers one question: **did it make the action
  fail?** If targets were named, the question is asked once per target, because a
  reaction can save one person and not another. A failed action does not happen, but
  **its cost stays spent**. Pressing use was the decision, and the reaction only undid the
  consequence.

**You cannot hold your turn or set a trigger for later.** The way to act at the right
moment is Anticipate: bank Action Points as Reaction Points on your turn and spend them on
somebody else's when the moment comes.

> **At the table.** Kaelen casts Bramble Whip at an enemy standing next to Dorn. Before
> her dice are thrown, everyone with a Reaction Point is asked whether they want to react.
>
> Dorn answers first, which closes the question for everybody else. Kaelen's roll waits.
> Dorn spends one Reaction Point and one Willpower on Intercept, a Guardian card that puts
> him in the way of a hit meant for somebody next to him. His card resolves in full.
>
> Now the table answers: did that make Kaelen's spell fail? It did. The vine wraps around
> Dorn's shield instead of its target and does nothing. Kaelen's two Action Points and her
> Willpower are gone all the same.

### 5.5 The basic actions

Every character can take all of these, whatever they are and whatever they hold. They are
cards on your Character tab like everything else.

| Action | Cost | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Move** | 1 AP | Move up to your Movement Speed |
| **Climb** | 1 AP | Climb a vertical surface, up to half your Movement Speed. The Game Master may ask for a Roll |
| **Jump** | 2 AP | Jump up to your Movement Speed across, or your own height up |
| **Investigate** | 2 AP | Mind or Instinct Roll against an enemy's Grit, or against a DC the Game Master sets. On a success you learn something useful: a weakness, a strength, a hazard in the room |
| **Hide** | 2 AP | Only when no enemy can see you. Instinct Roll against the Grit of every enemy. Each enemy you beat loses sight of you. You stay hidden until the end of a turn in which you do anything |
| **Grapple** | 2 AP | Physique Roll against the target's Reflex. On a success the target is grappled |
| **Shove** | 2 AP | Physique Attack Roll. On a hit, push the target 1.5 metres (one space) or knock it prone |
| **Skill Check** | The Game Master sets it, usually 0 AP | Attempt something that is not an attack and could fail. Choose the attribute the task calls for and roll against a DC the Game Master sets. Any skill you hold that applies is added to the roll and pays its own price |
| **Interact** | The Game Master sets it | Handle something in the world: a door, a lever, an object on the floor |
| **Anticipate** | Converts | Move any number of Action Points into Reaction Points, one for one. Nothing is spent |
| **Stabilize** | 4 AP | Mind Roll against a dying character's negative Health divided by 5. On a success they stop dying and sit at 0 Health |
| **Inventory** | 4 AP | Take something out of your pack, put something in or swap. The belt is free to reach |

For Skill Check and Interact the sheet asks how many Action Points the Game Master ruled
before it spends anything. Most Skill Checks cost nothing.

### 5.6 Attacking

A card that is rolled against another character has one of two target numbers, and there
is no third.

- **An Attack Roll** is rolled against the target's **Defense**. Weapon attacks and spells
  that say "Attack" work this way.
- **An Attribute Roll** is rolled against the target's **Reflex** or **Grit**, whichever
  the card names. Spells and effects that are resisted rather than dodged work this way.

Either way, you succeed if your total equals or exceeds the number.

### 5.6.1 Martial Moves

A **Martial Move** is a trained manoeuvre taught by some talent sets. It is not an action
of its own. You **add it to a weapon attack** as you make the attack.

- You choose the move before you pay. The attack's cost and the move's cost are paid
  together, and everything the move changes about the attack is settled before the dice
  are rolled. On the sheet, moves you could add appear as tick boxes in the attack's own
  window.
- **A move costs Willpower.** The Action Points belong to the attack it rides on.
- **One move per attack.** Some Master talents allow two.
- A move can only be added to the weapon's plain attack, until a talent widens it to the
  weapon's special attack.
- The move rides on the attempt, not the result. The Willpower is spent whether you hit
  or miss, and the part of the move that says "on a hit" is lost on a miss.

Which moves you know is a hand you choose from your set's pool, the way a caster prepares
spells, and a Long Rest lets you change it.

### 5.7 Damage, in order

When damage lands on a body, it is applied in this order, for each separate hit:

1. **Armor** is subtracted first. Armor applies to every hit separately: three hits of 6
   against Armor 2 are 12 damage through, not 16.
2. **Shield** absorbs what is left, until it runs out.
3. **Health** takes the rest.

> **At the table.** The Blightgeist has Armor 2 and 5 points of Shield, and Kaelen's 21
> Sharp damage arrives.
>
> Armor comes off first: 21 minus 2 is **19 through**. The Shield absorbs 5 of that and is
> gone. The remaining **14 comes off its Health**.
>
> If the card had landed three separate hits of 7 instead of one hit of 21, Armor would
> have come off each of them: three times (7 minus 2) is **15 through**, not 19. Many small
> hits do worse against armour than one heavy blow.

### 5.8 The damage types

There are nine damage types, in three families of three.

| Family | Types |
| --- | --- |
| **Physical** | Sharp, Blunt, Force |
| **Elemental** | Fire, Cold, Lightning |
| **Metaphysical** | Sacred, Decay, Psychic |

Every card that deals damage names one of these. A weapon's damage type is printed on the
weapon, and an enchantment can change it. Poison is a **condition** (see the glossary),
not a damage type.

### 5.9 Vulnerable and Resistant

Both apply to one damage type at a time.

- **Vulnerable** to a type: you take **double** damage from it.
- **Resistant** to a type: you take **half** damage from it.
- Neither stacks with itself. Two sources of vulnerability to Fire still mean double, not
  quadruple. Two resistances still mean half.
- They cancel each other. Vulnerable and Resistant to the same type means you take the
  damage as written.

### 5.10 Cover

**Total cover** is something solid entirely between you and the target. It breaks line of
sight, and nothing that needs to see the target can reach it.

**Anything less than total cover is partial cover**, and there is one rule for all of it:
attacks against a target in partial cover are made with **Disadvantage**. A low wall, a
doorway, a body in the way and a moving cart all count the same.

### 5.11 The world hurting you

Falling, drowning, fire, a collapsing floor. Environmental damage comes in **three
bands** rather than a table with a row for every hazard, and the Game Master picks the one
that fits. Each band is a flat amount scaled to the level of the character it happens to.

| Band | Damage | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Minor** | 5 x level | A short fall, a scald, a lungful of smoke |
| **Serious** | 10 x level | A long fall, standing in a fire, a ceiling coming down |
| **Deadly** | 20 x level | A cliff, a furnace, the bottom of a lift shaft |

The damage is a flat amount. No dice are rolled for it, nothing explodes and criticals
play no part. Armor and Shield still apply in the usual order (see 5.7).

Whether the damage lands at all is a separate question. A fall lands. A hazard a character
might get clear of, such as a trap or a rockfall, is first rolled against their Reflex, and
one they might hold out against, such as poison in the air, against their Grit (see 1.4 and
11.2.1). The damage is dealt only if that roll succeeds.

Against the Health a character of that level actually has, using their best attribute for
Physique:

| | Minor | Serious | Deadly |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Level 1** (70 Health) | 5 | 10 | 20 |
| **Level 5** (130) | 25 | 50 | 100 |
| **Level 9** (190) | 45 | 90 | 180 |
| **Level 12** (230) | 60 | 120 | 240 |

The world gets more dangerous as you level, not less. A cliff takes a third of a level 1
character's Health and all of a level 12's. That is deliberate: monsters scale with you,
and the world still has to be worth respecting.

> **Designer's note.** Scaling to level is a ruling. The three multipliers are proposals
> and can be changed. If Deadly should not outright kill a character at the cap, 15 x
> level tops out at 180 against 230 Health.

### 5.12 Healing

Healing raises Health, up to your maximum. Shield can also be granted, up to your Shield
cap.

**Nothing heals on its own.** Health does not come back with time. It comes back from
three places only:

- **A rest.** A Short Rest returns half your maximum Health. A Long Rest returns all of it.
  Both cost Supplies (see Chapter Eight).
- **A card.** Any spell or ability that says it heals.
- **A potion or field care.** A potion anyone can drink, or a skill that lets somebody
  treat you in the field.

A party with no Supplies and no potions does not get better. That is the pressure the
Supply pool is for.

## Chapter Six · Magic

### 6.1 The schools

Spells are grouped in three schools, and each school in families.

| School | Families |
| --- | --- |
| **Primal** | Flora, Wild, Life, Blood, Death |
| **Elemental** | Fire, Water, Wind, Lightning, Earth, Magma, Storm, Mud |
| **Ethereal** | Light, Shadow, Time, Spacial |

The Elemental school has four base elements (Fire, Water, Wind and Earth). Pairing them
gives six more families, of which three are written so far: Magma, Storm and Mud.

A **Unique** spell belongs to a single item rather than a school. Whoever holds the item
can cast it, and nobody can learn it any other way.

Which spells you can learn is set by your talent set. A set names a school, or a family,
and how many spells you know at each rank. Some sets let you change your prepared spells
at a Long Rest.

### 6.2 The rungs

Spells come in four rungs: **Novice**, **Adept**, **Master** and **Legendary**. A talent
set says which rungs it reaches at each rank.

### 6.3 What a spell costs

A spell costs Action Points and Willpower, both printed on the card. A spell's attribute
is usually Mind. A card may name another attribute or say that it uses your highest.

### 6.4 The four second halves

Most spells carry an optional second part under a second heading. The four names mean
four different things:

- **Overcast**: spend more than the spell asks to make it do more. It is always optional.
- **Multicast**: spend more to affect more targets.
- **Blood Tithe**: pay in Health instead of Willpower. The amount is always based on your
  Physique, whatever attribute you cast with.
- **Upkeep**: a cost paid at every one of your Turn Starts to keep the spell running. Miss
  one and the spell ends.

An Overcast is either part of the cast and priced on top of the printed cost, or a separate
spend on a spell that is already running and priced on its own. The opening words tell you
which: "When casting X" happens inside the cast, "While X is active" does not. The sheet
offers each second half as a box you can tick in the card's own window.

### 6.5 Empowered and Elevated

Two words that are easy to confuse:

- **Empowered** adds a die. 2d6 becomes 3d6.
- **Elevated** grows each die one size. A d6 becomes a d8. It stacks, and a die cannot
  grow past a d12.

## Chapter Seven · Gear and Coin

### 7.1 Where things go

| Place | How many |
| --- | --- |
| Armor | 3 slots: head, torso, legs |
| Weapons | 2: a primary and a secondary |
| Bag | 1 |
| Belt | 3 loops, up to 5 |
| Trinkets | No limit |
| Pack | No limit, but everything in it weighs |

Only the primary weapon's cards can be used. Swapping the secondary into the primary slot
in a fight costs 2 Action Points. Items on your belt can be reached freely. Getting into
your pack costs the Inventory action.

### 7.2 Armor

Armor comes in three families, and each family in three tiers: Common, Rare and Epic. A
piece of armor adds Armor, which is flat damage reduction. Some pieces add Defense too.

Filling **all three slots** with pieces from one family changes how your Defense is
calculated:

| Full set | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| **Light Armor** | Your Defense is equal to your Reflex |
| **Magic Armor** | Your Defense is based on your Grit |
| **Heavy Armor** | Your Defense is increased by half your Armor |

*Armor* and *Defense* are different numbers. Armor is subtracted from every hit that
lands. Defense is what an attack has to roll against to land at all.

Two rules settle how set bonuses combine with everything else:

- **All Armor counts towards Heavy's half.** Armor is one number wherever it came from, a
  breastplate, an enchantment or a talent, and the half is taken from the whole of it.
- **Flat Defense from gear adds on top of a set bonus.** A full Light set makes your
  Defense equal to your Reflex, and a shield's Defense is then added to that. A set bonus
  changes what Defense is built from. It does not stop other bonuses applying.

### 7.3 Weapons

A weapon is an item that teaches **two attack cards** while you hold it. Equip it and the
cards are yours. Take it off and they are gone.

Every weapon's plain attack follows one table: **what the attack costs in Action Points
decides what it deals.**

| Cost | Damage |
| --- | --- |
| 2 AP | `1d6 + attribute` |
| 3 AP | `2d6 + attribute` |
| 4 AP | `2d6 + 2 x attribute` |
| 5 AP | `3d6 + 2 x attribute` |
| 6 AP | `3d6 + 3 x attribute` |

Four kinds of weapon read that table differently:

- **A weapon with a shield** deals the damage of the row below its cost, for 1 more Action
  Point, and the shield adds Armor and Defense while it is held.
- **Paired weapons** deal the row below with d4s instead of d6s, rolled twice, for 1 more
  Action Point.
- **Crossbows** deal their own row for 1 Action Point less, and must be reloaded between
  shots.
- **Firearms** deal their own row for 1 Action Point, and carry a magazine that runs out.

The attribute a weapon uses is printed on the weapon. A heavy weapon uses Physique, a
quick one uses Instinct, and the wielder does not get to choose.

### 7.4 Bags

A bag raises the weight you can carry. It does nothing else. There are seven, and every
one costs 20 coins per kilogram it adds.

| Capacity added | Cost |
| --- | --- |
| 5 kg | 100 coins |
| 10 kg | 200 |
| 15 kg | 300 |
| 20 kg | 400 |
| 30 kg | 600 |
| 40 kg | 800 |
| 50 kg | 1,000 |

The three smallest are ordinary craft bags and get heavier as they get bigger. The four
above them are **Pockets**, magical pouches folded through themselves, and they get
lighter as they get bigger: the largest holds fifty kilograms and weighs three hundred
grams. A bag's own weight counts against the room it gives you.

### 7.5 Charges and uses

A card or an item that can only be used a certain number of times says so on its face,
and names the rest that refills it. "Once per Long Rest" and "three times per Long Rest"
are the same rule with a different number. The sheet counts the uses and refills them
when you take the named rest.

### 7.6 Money

**Ten coins are worth about one euro.** That is the whole conversion. A Game Master can
price anything in the world by thinking of what it would cost at home and adding a zero.

A character starts with **2,000 to 6,000 coins** depending on their background (see 4.4),
which is two to six hundred euros: enough to be equipped, nowhere near enough to be well
armoured. A Healing Potion costs 200 coins, a Common weapon
4,000 and a piece of Rare armor 7,000. Gear is the expensive thing in this world, and
living is cheap. Everyday prices for the Game Master to work from are in 11.9.

### 7.7 Supplies

Supplies are one number standing for everything a traveller uses up: rations, powder,
reagents, rope, oil, thread. Counting them one item at a time is exactly what the number
exists to avoid.

- **Supplies weigh nothing** and take no room in your pack.
- **One Supply costs 10 coins**, about a euro.
- **A character starts with 70**, enough for seven nights on the road.
- **Rests cost Supplies.** A Short Rest costs 5 and a Long Rest 10. Crafting during a rest
  costs more.

A Long Rest is the only way to recover Willpower, and one of the few ways to recover
Health (see 5.12), so running out of Supplies is what stops a party recovering. That is
the pressure they are designed to create.

### 7.8 Buying and selling

Every item on the website has a printed cost in coins, and that is its price in the game.

The website has no shop. Buying is a conversation with the Game Master: find somebody
selling what you want, agree the price and then record it on your sheet. Take the coins
off in your coin ledger, and add the item to your pack from the Inventory tab, where the
codex browser lists everything in the game. Items your Game Master has made up can be
added as a written entry. Selling works the same way in reverse. What somebody will pay
for a used breastplate is the Game Master's call.

## Chapter Eight · Rest and Downtime

There are two rests. Both are taken from the Character tab, and the sheet shows you
everything a rest is about to change before you confirm it.

| | Short Rest | Long Rest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Time | 1 hour | 8 hours |
| Supplies | 5 | 10 |
| Health | Half your maximum back | All of it |
| Willpower | Nothing | All of it |
| Shield | Left in place | All of it removed |
| Ends | Effects that last "until a Short Rest" | Those, and effects that last "until a Long Rest" |
| Actions | None | **One** |

A Long Rest ends everything a Short Rest ends, and never the other way round. An effect
written "until your next Long Rest" survives a Short Rest.

Both rests also refill whatever was waiting on one: a flask's charges, a card's uses, a
prepared hand of spells.

A rest you cannot pay for does not happen. No Supplies are spent and nothing is restored.

### 8.1 The Long Rest action

A Long Rest buys **one** action, chosen when you take the rest. What is on offer depends on
what your character can do:

- **Preparing**: a caster who chooses their spells from a pool re-chooses them.
- **Working**: several background skills are work done during a Long Rest, paid for in
  Supplies. Foraging around the camp, keeping watch, crafting.
- **Enchanting**: an Enchanter lays a working (see 9.2).
- **Brewing**: an Alchemist turns Supplies into potions (see 9.3).

Some skills and enchantments make rests cheaper. A skill and an enchantment are different
sources, so their reductions add together.

### 8.2 Where you sleep

A rest on the road costs your own Supplies. A rest under a roof is **paid for in coin
instead**, and a better roof gives a better night.

| | Coins | What the Long Rest gives, on top of the usual |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A common room** | 100 | Nothing extra. A bed, a door and no Supplies spent |
| **A good house** | 400 | **1 Karma** |
| **A fine house** | 1,200 | 1 Karma, and you start the day with **Shield equal to your Mind** |

Prices are per person per night, and they replace the rest's Supplies cost. Karma is still
capped at one per level, so a good house is worth most to somebody who has spent theirs.

Sleeping rough never costs coin and always costs Supplies.

> **Designer's note.** The two rewards are rulings. The three prices and the amount of
> Shield are proposals.

### 8.3 Downtime

A rest is a night. **A downtime turn is a week**, and it is how the calendar moves between
one adventure and the next. A downtime turn buys **one activity**, the way a Long Rest
buys one action:

| Activity | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Earn** | Work at what your background knows. **500 coins**, or **1,000** if a skill or background you hold plainly covers the work |
| **Study** | Learn something the party needs to know. The Game Master decides what can be learned and may ask for a Roll |
| **Make** | Craft, brew or enchant at a scale one night cannot reach: **seven** Long Rest actions' worth of the same work, paid for as seven |
| **Recover** | Shake off something a rest cannot end. What qualifies is the Game Master's call |
| **Stand** | Spend the week on people: a contact made, a favour earned, a reputation built or repaired |

A downtime turn assumes food and a roof, so it costs a week's board (600 coins) or the
Supplies to camp for seven nights. If it is interrupted, the week is spent and nothing
comes of it.

> **Designer's note.** Everything in this section is a proposal except the idea of it.
> Every number here can be changed.

## Chapter Nine · Making Things

### 9.1 The same-source rule

**An effect does not stack with itself when it comes from the same source.** The same
enchantment on two rings gives you its bonus once. A bonus from a lineage and the same
bonus from an enchantment are different sources, and both count. The sheet applies this
rule wherever bonuses are added up.

### 9.2 Enchanting

An enchantment is a working laid on a person or on a piece of gear. Its price is set by the
**Magic Burden** it carries:

- **70 Supplies** per point of Burden, when an Enchanter lays it during a Long Rest.
- **750 coins** per point of Burden, when it is bought.

An Ephemeral Enchantment lasts an hour and is never stored on the sheet, because a stored
bonus would have no way of wearing off.

**Magic Burden is the only limit.** There is no cap on how many enchantments an Enchanter
may lay and no waiting time between them. What stops the pile growing forever is the
wearer: Burden is a hard ceiling on what a body can carry (see 2.7), so the limit is on
wearing magic rather than on making it.

### 9.3 Brewing

An Alchemist turns Supplies into potions during a Long Rest, at **10 Supplies per point of
Willpower** the potion costs to drink. The Willpower printed on a potion is what sets both
its brewing cost and its price in coin (see 9.6).

### 9.4 Brews

A Brew is the Cauldron Keeper's craft, and it is a different thing from a potion. A Brew is
made on the spot from Ingredients:

- **at least 1 Essence**, which decides what the Brew does,
- **exactly 1 Catalyst**, which decides who it reaches,
- **any number of Infusions** stirred in on top.

A Brew costs the combined Action Point and Willpower cost of everything in it, and it takes
effect immediately. A Keeper learns Novice Ingredients at Rank 1, Adept at Rank 2 and
Master at Rank 3.

### 9.5 Forging

Forging makes an item rather than finding one: a base item plus an enchantment, named by
whoever made it. The result is an item of its own, which is what lets two silver rings
carry different enchantments and different names.

**Anyone may forge, anywhere, if they can pay.** No talent, skill or workshop is needed.
The cost is the whole of the limit, so forging is closer to commissioning a made thing than
to working at a bench. On the website the forge is behind **Make an Enchanted Item** in
the Inventory tab's codex browser, and a made item can be shared with another player as a
code.

### 9.6 Potions

A potion is bottled magic, and the point of it is that **anybody can drink one**. No
talent, school, attribute or card is needed. A party with no caster can still buy the
effect.

**Drinking a potion costs Willpower**, and Action Points, exactly as its card says. The
Willpower printed on the potion sets everything about it: what the drinker pays, what the
potion costs in coin (100 coins per point, with a surcharge at Adept and Master) and what
an Alchemist pays in Supplies to brew one.

## Chapter Ten · Creatures

### 10.1 The three ranks

Every creature has a rank, and the rank sets how much it can do in a turn.

| Rank | Action Points | Reaction Points | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Minion** | 6 | 0 | Quick to kill and cannot react at all. Dangerous in numbers |
| **General** | 6 | 6 | As capable as a player character, with its own Willpower |
| **Overlord** | 12 | 12 | The centre of a fight. Gains **3 Reaction Points every time a player takes a turn** |

### 10.2 Creatures scale

A creature is not a fixed block of numbers. It is a shape: which attribute is its main one,
which is its second, a few adjustments of its own and its cards. The Game Master picks a
level when they put it in a fight, and the level turns the shape into a full set of stats.
The same creature can be dropped into a level 3 fight or a level 9 one.

The curve is the character's own:

- **main attribute**: 4 + 2, then +1 at every odd level from 3
- **second attribute**: 4 + 1, then +1 at every odd level from 3
- **third attribute**: 4

plus the creature's own adjustments, which are what make one slow and enormous and another
frail. Reflex, Grit, Initiative and Shield are calculated the same way a character's are,
because the players' cards roll against them.

Every creature carries a suggested experience value per level. The bestiary on the
website lists the printed creatures, three of each rank. A Game Master with a paid account
can build their own (see 11.6).

## Chapter Eleven · Running the Game

This chapter is for the Game Master. The players' rules are in the chapters before it.
This one explains what the Game Master decides, and how to use the campaign tools on the
website.

### 11.1 What the Game Master does

- **Describe the world** and play everyone in it who is not a player character.
- **Decide when a roll is needed.** If something is certain, or its failure would not be
  interesting, it just happens. Ask for a Roll when the outcome is in doubt and both
  outcomes lead somewhere.
- **Set the difficulty** from the ladder in 1.10, agree which attribute a Skill Check is
  rolled with, and set the Action Point cost of Skill Checks and Interact actions.
- **Call results** when there was no DC, and answer the reaction question: did the
  reaction make the action fail?
- **Run the creatures**: their turns, their reactions and their damage.
- **Roll for the world** when it acts on a character: a trap against their Reflex, a poison
  against their Grit (see 11.2.1).
- **Hand out experience, Karma and loot.**
- **Rule on anything the rules do not cover.** When you do, say so and write it down so it
  is the same next time.

### 11.2 Setting a difficulty

Use the ladder in 1.10. Pick the word first and read the number off it: is this Easy,
Moderate or Hard for a competent person? Only then look at the table of odds. A level 1
character rolling their best attribute succeeds at Moderate less than half the time, and
almost never at Hard without help.

Some guidance:

- **Most things are Easy or Moderate.** Hard should feel hard. Save Demanding and above for
  moments the whole table remembers.
- **Set the DC before the roll**, and say it out loud. The sheet asks for it when the player
  rolls, and a number given after the dice land is not a difficulty.
- **Agree the attribute with the player.** The task does not fix it. How the character
  goes about the task does. Ask what they are doing, and the answer names the attribute: a
  lock opened by understanding the mechanism is Mind, and the same lock opened by feel is
  Instinct. A threat is Physique when it is made with size, Mind when it is made with
  exactly the right words and Instinct when it is made with nerve. The table in 1.3 says
  what each attribute stands for. If the player's answer is a stretch, say so and settle
  on one before the dice are thrown.
- **A skill is not a bonus you add.** A player who holds a relevant skill ticks it on their
  own sheet, and the sheet applies what the skill grants. Your job is to say whether the
  skill applies.
- **If you cannot decide on a DC**, let the roll go without one. The sheet shows four
  buttons and you call the result.

### 11.2.1 When the world rolls

A trap, a poison, a rockfall a character might get clear of: these are Rolls you make
against the character's Reflex or Grit (see 1.4). A creature's card already says what it
adds to its roll. A danger without a card needs a number from you.

- **Give the danger a level.** Usually that is the level of the characters it is meant
  for. Add what a creature's main attribute is at that level (see 10.2): 6 at level 1, and
  one more at every odd level from 3. A trap set for a level 5 party rolls `2d6 + 8`.
- **Roll it from the dice tray** as an Attribute Roll, with that number typed into the
  modifier box and the character's Reflex or Grit as the DC. The result is written to the
  table log like any other Roll, so the player sees what they were up against.
- **On a success the danger lands.** Deal its damage from the bands in 5.11, or apply its
  condition. On a failure the character got clear, or held.

> **Designer's note.** Rolling a danger as a creature of its level is a proposal. It lands
> about as often as a creature's own spell does against the same number. A danger that
> should be harder to escape can be given a higher level than the party.

### 11.3 Action Point costs you set

Two basic actions have no printed cost, and the player's sheet asks how much you ruled
before it spends anything.

- **Skill Check.** Most cost nothing. Charge 1 or 2 Action Points when the attempt takes
  real time in the middle of a fight: picking a lock while arrows fly, reading an
  inscription under fire.
- **Interact.** Opening an unlocked door is free or 1 Action Point. Working a heavy
  mechanism might be 2 or 3.

### 11.4 Running a fight

At the table a fight runs like this:

1. Call for Initiative. Everyone rolls, and a surprised side rolls with Disadvantage.
2. Take turns from the highest total down. On a creature's turn, spend its Action Points on
   its cards the way a player would. Minions cannot react at all. Overlords gain 3 Reaction
   Points every time a player takes a turn.
3. When a player acts, offer the reaction window. If a creature with Reaction Points wants
   to react, it competes for the one reaction like everyone else.
4. Apply damage in the order in 5.7: Armor, Shield, Health.
5. End the fight when it is over. Award experience if you count it.

Hazards deal damage from the bands in 5.11. Pick Minor, Serious or Deadly and multiply by
the character's level. No dice are rolled for the damage. Whether a hazard can be avoided
at all is a Roll against the character's Reflex or Grit (see 11.2.1).

### 11.5 The campaign on the website

Everything on the website that belongs to the Game Master is in a **campaign**. Your
Campaigns page lists the campaigns you run and the campaigns you play in.

- **Create a campaign** from your Campaigns page. It gets a name, a description and a
  picture, and a **join code** such as `KQ2M-8VXR`. How many campaigns you can run depends
  on your account tier.
- **Seat the players.** Give them the join code. On their own Campaigns page they press
  **Join With a Code**, pick which of their characters sits at your table and they are
  in. You can also paste a link to a character's sheet on the campaign's Details tab. You
  can retire the code and mint a fresh one at any time.
- **The Overview tab** shows one block per character at the table: their pools, their
  stats and their creatures, live, as they change. It is read only. The Game Master never
  writes to a player's sheet.
- **The Table Log** is on the campaign page and on the Character tab of every sheet at the
  table. Every card played, every roll, every rest and every turn is written there as it
  happens, with the dice shown. It is the table's record, and it is also how anything you
  do reaches the players: damage, effects and turn calls travel through the log, and each
  player's own sheet applies what is addressed to them.
- **The Bestiary and Encounters tabs** appear only for the Game Master.

### 11.6 Building an encounter

An encounter is a group of enemies you prepare, before the session or during it.

- On the **Encounters** tab, create an encounter and give it a name and notes.
- **Add enemies** from the bestiary. A control called **Add at** sets the level they come in
  at. It opens on the party's own average level, and you can step it up or down for an
  easier or harder fight. Repeats are numbered, so three of the same creature are 1., 2.
  and 3.
- Under each creature the shelf prints a **difficulty line**: its rank, its level and its
  suggested experience at that level.
- Each enemy in the encounter is a block showing its stats, its pools and its cards, with
  buttons to adjust Health and Shield by hand and a switch for any ward it carries.
- **Build your own creature** in the Bestiary tab's forge. You set its name, type, rank,
  main and second attribute, armor family and cards, and it can learn any card in the
  game. Forging is a paid-account feature.

### 11.7 Running a fight with the tool

Open the encounter when the fight begins.

1. **Roll Initiative.** The tool rolls for your enemies and asks every player at the table
   to roll on their own sheet. A prompt appears for them with an Advantage stepper, in
   case a side is surprised. When everyone has rolled the fight starts by itself. If
   somebody is away, **Roll the rest and start** rolls for them. **Call it off** cancels.
2. **The order** appears as a row of chips, one per body, showing Health as a bar and
   whoever is up lit. Each player is told when it is their turn, and presses **Start my
   turn** on their own sheet.
3. **On an enemy's turn**, play its cards from its block. Pick its targets when the card
   asks. The dice roll on the table and land in the log, signed by the enemy.
4. **Land the damage.** When a roll settles, a window opens over the picked targets showing
   the arithmetic for each body: Armor off, Shield absorbs, the rest to Health. Press
   **Land it**. Enemies are updated on the spot. Damage to players is delivered to their
   sheets and applied there. Effects a card inflicts are tracked the same way, on whoever
   they landed on.
5. **Turn boundaries.** When a turn starts or ends and something is waiting on it, an
   Upkeep or a condition that ticks, the tool stops and asks. **Roll it** rolls the dice,
   **Take it** applies a stated number.
6. **Reactions.** When a player acts, a six-second window opens for everyone who could
   answer, your enemies with Reaction Points included. One reaction resolves, then the
   tool asks whether the action failed, per target if there were several.
7. **Next** moves the order on after an enemy's turn. A player's own **End Turn** moves it
   on for them. Overlords are paid their 3 Reaction Points automatically when a player's
   turn comes up.
8. **End the fight** when it is over. The order clears and running effects stay.

**Sharing what the players see.** Each encounter has a switch that shows the players enemy
Health and Shield bars and what is running on each enemy. It is off by default. Turning it
on also shares your notes on that encounter.

### 11.8 Experience, Karma and loot

The Game Master cannot write to a player's sheet, so rewards are handed over in words and
the players record them.

- **Experience.** Tell the table the number. Each player enters it in the ledger behind
  their level badge, which also shows how far they are from the next level. If you play by
  milestones, tell them the level and they set it directly.
- **Karma.** Give it for good play, for a good idea or for a memorable failure. The
  player adds it on their sheet, and the sheet caps it at one per level.
- **Coins and Supplies.** Say what was found. The player adds it in the matching ledger,
  with a note.
- **Items.** Anything in the game's codex can be added to a pack from the Inventory tab. A
  made item can be handed over as a share code, so an enchanted blade you built for the
  dragon's hoard arrives on the player's sheet exactly as you made it. Anything the codex
  does not have can be written in as a custom entry.

### 11.9 Pricing the world

Every item has a printed price. For everything else, ten coins are about a euro. Think of
what it would cost at home and add a zero.

| | Coins | About |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A plain meal | 50 | 5 euro |
| A good meal | 200 | 20 euro |
| A letter carried a day's ride | 100 | 10 euro |
| A day's passage by coach | 300 | 30 euro |
| A week's board, nothing fancy | 600 | 60 euro |
| A bored watchman's silence | 500 | 50 euro |

Lodging is in 8.2, because where the party sleeps changes what a rest gives them.

> **Designer's note.** The euro anchor is a ruling. The six rows above are proposals.

### 11.10 Rulings and the open questions

Where a rule is silent, rule and move on. Write the ruling down, in the campaign's
description or in your own notes, so it holds next session. Appendix B lists the numbers
in this book that are still proposals, so you know which ones you are free to change
without contradicting anything.

## Chapter Twelve · The Glossary

Every defined term in the game. A term means exactly this wherever it is printed, and on
the website you can point at any of them on a card to read this text.

### What you spend

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Willpower** | Your reserve of nerve and focus. Spent to power abilities and to bend rolls in your favour. Comes back on a Long Rest. |
| **Action Points** | What a turn is made of. Every action costs some, and you get six back at the start of each of your turns. |
| **Reaction Points** | Earned during a round and spent outside your own turn. A reaction can be paid from these instead of your Action Points. |
| **reaction** | Something you do on somebody else's turn, in answer to what they did. Costs the same as it would on your turn. You just pay it from a different pool. |
| **Karma** | Spend 1 after seeing a die result to add 1d4 to it. |
| **Supplies** | The crate you travel on: rations, powder, reagents, rope. Spent on the road and on anything crafted during a rest. |
| **Coins** | Money. What you spend on people rather than on the road. |
| **Sacrifice** | When Sacrificing a resource or value (such as Health or Willpower), you ignore all reduction, mitigation or prevention effects to directly subtract the indicated amount. |

### What you have

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Health** | How much you have left. Past zero you are bleeding out, and a second full bar of damage kills you outright. |
| **Shield** | Damage soaked before it reaches Health, and whatever it cannot absorb carries straight through. Caps at half your maximum Health unless an effect says otherwise. A Long Rest takes whatever is left of it. |
| **bleeding out** | At 0 Health or below. You are still on the table, and a second full bar of damage is death. |
| **Defense** | How hard you are to hit. Every attack is rolled against it. |
| **Armor** | Flat damage reduction, taken off every hit that lands. It comes from gear alone. |
| **Reflex** | How fast you answer sudden danger: diving clear of a blast. Physique + Instinct. |
| **Grit** | How well you withstand what gets inside you: poison, fear, a mental attack. Instinct + Mind. |
| **Initiative** | Added to your roll for turn order when a fight starts. |
| **Movement Speed** | The maximum distance in meters (or feet) you can cover when taking the Move or Jump action. |
| **Jump Distance** | The maximum horizontal distance in meters (or feet) you can cover when taking the Jump action. |
| **Jump Height** | The maximum vertical distance in meters (or feet) you can clear when taking the Jump action. |
| **Magic Burden** | How much worked magic you can carry before it starts to weigh. Capacity is Level + Mind + 10. |

### Rolling

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Attack Roll** | A Roll made to hit a target. The attack succeeds if the total result equals or exceeds the target's Defense or targeted Attribute. |
| **skill check** | A Roll made to determine the success or failure of a non-combat or specialized task, adding the appropriate Attribute to the result. |
| **contested roll** | Both sides roll and the higher result wins. |
| **advantage** | When making a roll with Advantage, roll a d4 and add the value to your total result. Advantage stacks (each instance adds an additional d4). Advantage and Disadvantage cancel each other out on a 1-to-1 basis. |
| **disadvantage** | When making a roll with Disadvantage, roll a d4 and subtract the value from your total result. Disadvantage stacks (each instance adds an additional d4 subtraction). Disadvantage and Advantage cancel each other out on a 1-to-1 basis. |
| **reroll** | Roll the same check again and take the new result, whether it is better or worse. |
| **Critical Hit** | A Roll that lands 6 or more above the DC. It guarantees a hit and maximizes the damage dealt, and a maximized damage die explodes the way any other one does. A Roll that lands 6 or more below the DC is a Critical Failure. |
| **Exploding Dice** | When you roll the maximum value on a damage or healing die, that die explodes: roll an additional die of the category above and add it to the total. An 8 on a d8 throws a d10. A die that explodes into another maximum explodes again, and a d12 throws another d12. |
| **Empowered** | Increases the total number of your Damage Dice and Healing Dice by 1. Whenever a spell or ability calls for rolling dice, you roll 1 additional die of that same type (2d6 becomes 3d6). |
| **Elevated** | Increases the die size category of your Damage Dice and Healing Dice by one step (a d6 becomes a d8). Elevate stacks, but cannot increase a die size beyond a d12. |
| **Damage Dice** | Refers to the dice you indicated by ability to deal damage. |
| **Healing Dice** | The specific dice indicated by an ability or spell used to determine Health restored. |

### Attacking

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Melee Attack** | An attack made within reach, rolled against the target's Defense. |
| **Ranged Attack** | An attack made at distance, rolled against the target's Defense. |
| **weapon attack** | Either of the two attacks the weapon in your hands teaches you. |
| **Martial Move** | A trained manoeuvre bought with a talent, used alongside a weapon attack rather than instead of one. |
| **Melee** | An attack or spell range targeting entities that are directly adjacent to you or within physical reach. |
| **Ranged** | An attack or spell range targeting entities at a distance up to the maximum specified range in meters (or feet). |

### Position

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **entity** | Anything that can be targeted: a person, a beast, a construct, a thing that is awake. |
| **touch** | Refers to an entity or object adjacent to you that you can physically touch. |
| **reach** | As far as you can strike without moving your feet. Yours unless the thing in your hands says otherwise. |
| **see** | Refers to an entity, object or location to which you have a direct and clear line of sight. |
| **line of sight** | An unbroken view of the target. Total cover breaks it, and so does darkness. |
| **total cover** | Something solid entirely between you and it. Nothing that needs to see it can reach it. |

### Time

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Turn** | Indicates the active phase in combat assigned to a specific entity. Each turn features a Turn Start and Turn End. Effects measured in turns lose 1 turn of duration at each Turn Start. |
| **Turn Start** | The moment your turn opens, before you have spent anything. Upkeeps are paid here and effects that tick, tick here. |
| **Turn End** | The moment your turn closes, after everything you chose to do with it. |
| **Long Rest** | An 8-hour break that consumes 10 Supplies. Successfully completing a Long Rest fully restores your Health and Willpower, takes whatever Shield is left and allows you to perform 1 Long Rest Action. |
| **Short Rest** | A 1-hour break that consumes 5 Supplies. If completed uninterrupted, you regain half of your maximum Health. |

### Conditions

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **blinded** | The entity cannot see, as such cannot use any ability or spell that rely on being able to see. |
| **frightened** | Affected entities have Disadvantage on all Actions against the one frightening them. |
| **poisoned** | A Poisoned entity suffers Disadvantage on all actions until they complete a Long Rest or regain any amount of Health. |
| **diseased** | The afflicted entity receives -1 to all attributes until they take a Long Rest. |
| **burn** | The entity becomes vulnerable to Fire damage until they take a Short Rest. |
| **vulnerable** | A vulnerable entity takes double damage from that damage type. |
| **resistance** | A resistant entity takes half damage from that damage type. |
| **rooted** | A Rooted entity cannot take the Move or Jump action and is immune to any effect that would push or pull it. |
| **prone** | The target is on the ground, attacks are made with disadvantage and move action costs are doubled. When the entity uses a move action the prone condition ends. |
| **grappled** | A Grappled entity cannot use movement actions and has disadvantage on attacks. Once per turn a grappled entity can make a Physique roll against the grappler's Reflex. On a success they free themselves. |
| **stunned** | The entity cannot take Actions or Reactions until the effect ends. |
| **constrained** | Constrained entities cannot take actions but are still aware of their surroundings. |
| **interrupted** | The Action in question does not happen, but its cost is still spent. |
| **incapacitated** | An Incapacitated entity cannot move, take Actions or spend Reaction Points. |
| **marked** | Singled out. Whoever marked it knows where it is and hits it more easily. |
| **asleep** | Out cold, and unaware. Any damage wakes you, and so does an entity spending an action to shake you. |
| **unconscious** | Down and unaware. You act at all only once somebody brings you round. *(provisional)* |
| **dying** | Below 0 Health and going. Stabilize stops it, and a second full bar of damage ends it. |
| **Bleed** | Stackable effect. The entity takes 1d6 damage at every one of its Turn Starts. It loses one stack whenever it receives healing. |
| **Wound** | Singular effect. Weapon attacks made against the entity are Empowered. It lasts until the entity receives healing or takes a rest. |
| **Corpse Carrion** | The afflicted entity takes the caster's attribute in Decay damage at every one of its Turn Starts. |

### Casting

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Overcast** | Spend more than the spell asks to make it do more. Always optional, always your call. |
| **Multicast** | Spend more than the spell asks to catch more targets with it. |
| **Upkeep** | A toll paid at every Turn Start to keep a spell running. Miss one and the spell ends there. |
| **Blood Tithe** | A price paid in Health rather than Willpower, and always in Physique whatever you cast with. |

### Brewing

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Brew** | Ingredients combined to unleash a magical effect: at least 1 Essence, exactly 1 Catalyst and any number of Infusions. It costs the combined Action Point and Willpower cost of everything in it, and takes effect immediately. |
| **Ingredient** | What a Brew is made of. A Keeper gains access to Novice Ingredients at Rank 1, Adept at Rank 2 and Master at Rank 3. |
| **Essence** | The Ingredient that decides what a Brew does. Every Brew needs at least one, and Improved Recipes allows two that are not the same. |
| **Catalyst** | The Ingredient that decides who a Brew reaches. Exactly one in every Brew. |
| **Infusion** | An Ingredient stirred in on top. A Brew may take any number of them. |

## Appendix A · The one page

The quick reference, in the order things come up at the table.

**The roll.** 2d6 + attribute, against a DC. A Skill Check, an Attack Roll and an Attribute
Roll are all this roll.

| Your total, compared to the DC | Result |
| --- | --- |
| 6 or more under | **Critical Failure** |
| Under | **Failure** |
| Equal or over | **Success** |
| 6 or more over | **Critical Success**. An attack hits and its damage is maximized |

**The ladder.** Easy 10 · Moderate 14 · Hard 18 · Demanding 22 · Formidable 26 · Mythic
30. Nothing above 22 can be reached by a bare roll.

**What to roll against.** An attack goes against **Defense**. A spell that is not an attack
goes against **Reflex** or **Grit**, and so does a trap or a poison. A Skill Check goes
against a DC from the ladder.

**Advantage** adds 1d4. **Disadvantage** subtracts 1d4. They stack and they cancel one for
one. **Karma** adds 1d4 after the roll, once per roll.

**A turn.** Six Action Points back at the start. Every running effect loses a turn. Upkeeps
are paid. Reaction Points are untouched.

**The basic actions.** Move 1 · Climb 1 · Jump 2 · Investigate 2 · Hide 2 · Grapple 2 ·
Shove 2 · Stabilize 4 · Inventory 4 · Interact and Anticipate variable · Skill Check as the
Game Master says, usually nothing.

**Damage lands in this order.** Armor off each hit, then Shield, then Health. Vulnerable
doubles, Resistant halves, neither stacks and the two cancel.

**The nine types.** Sharp, Blunt, Force · Fire, Cold, Lightning · Sacred, Decay, Psychic.

**Death.** 0 or below is bleeding out. Minus your maximum Health is dead. Stabilize is a
Mind Roll against their negative Health divided by 5.

**Rests.** Short: 1 hour, 5 Supplies, half your Health. Long: 8 hours, 10 Supplies, all
your Health and Willpower, all Shield removed, one Long Rest action.

**Distance.** 1.5 metres = 5 feet = 1 space.

**Money.** 10 coins are about a euro. A Supply is 10 coins.

**The world hurting you.** Minor 5 x level · Serious 10 x level · Deadly 20 x level.

## Appendix B · Notes on this edition

### What is a proposal

Every rule in this book is either how the game is played on the website or a ruling by its
designer. Five sets of numbers are proposals, written where a rule was settled and its
arithmetic was not. Each carries a Designer's note where it appears, and each can be
changed without contradicting anything else.

| | Where | The proposal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Environmental bands | 5.11 | Minor 5, Serious 10, Deadly 20, each times level |
| Lodging | 8.2 | 100 / 400 / 1,200 coins and Shield equal to Mind at the top |
| Downtime | 8.3 | A week, one activity, 500 coins for a week's work |
| Everyday prices | 11.9 | Six rows off the ten-coins-to-a-euro anchor |
| Dangers without a card | 11.2.1 | A trap or a poison rolls as a creature of its level: 2d6 plus that level's main attribute |

One glossary term is marked *(provisional)*: **unconscious**. Its wording is a best
reading of what the cards using it imply.

### What is not written yet

1. **There is no ability that raises the dead.** The price is set (see 2.3.1). The spell
   that does the work has not been written.
2. **Twenty-one talent sets are names without cards**: Brawler, Runebearer, Hemoturgy,
   Totemic, Painseeker, Dragon Aspect, Virtuoso, Flowing Fist, Sharpshooter, Wilder,
   Necromancer, Spellquill, Spellblade, Thaumaturge, Tactician, Elemental Aspect,
   Beastbond, Oathbound, Quartermaster, Weaver and Weapon Master.
3. **The world.** This is a rules book, and the setting is one sentence on the cover. It
   gets a book of its own.

The formulas, prices and glossary in this book are read from the game as it runs on the
website, and the website's rulebook page is rendered from this document. Where this book
